# Logo design rules

The rules every Ferry logo decision was checked against: the UCDA golden rules, how the reference board presents logos, and construction craft.

Apply to every logo, wordmark, lockup or brand-mark task. Run the checklist at the end before showing anything.

## 1. Before drawing (UCDA 1, 4, 6, 8)
- Groundwork: why the brand exists, what it does, who it's for, what sets it apart. Write it in one line first.
- Pick ONE emotion to evoke and say it in words before choosing type. Every type choice must trace back to it.
- Appropriateness: the form, type and colour must fit the brand's values and audience. Have a rationale you can say in one sentence.
- Difference: list what competitors all share (type style, palette, symbol on the left of the name) and break at least one of those patterns on purpose.
- Don't be too literal. Abstract marks last longer, and meaning builds through association.

## 2. Type and emotion
- Weight: bold/heavy reads sturdy and reliable; light reads delicate, premium or fragile.
- Shape: round, open curves read pleasant and warm (the best-supported finding in type research); sharp angles and tight apertures read tense, technical or aggressive.
- Classification: neo-grotesk = neutral and corporate; humanist or editorial grotesk = honest and warm; geometric = modern, engineered and cool; serif = heritage, trust and more emotional; high-contrast display serif = luxury and editorial; soft or wonky serif = crafted and friendly; script = personal, handmade.
- Width: wide reads stable, calm and horizon-like; condensed reads urgent and editorial.
- Case: lowercase reads approachable; caps read authoritative.
- Tracking: tight reads confident and compact; loose reads airy and premium.
- Never ship a default typeface as-is. Add at least one custom detail: a cut terminal, a ligature, an altered letter, or a shared line with the symbol (UCDA 9; Calendly, Fieldman, Messy Studio).

## 3. Construction (when a symbol locks up with a word)
- The symbol and the word are drawn as ONE object, not an icon placed next to text. Measure the reference: size the symbol against the ascender height, align it to real type lines (ascender, x-height, baseline, descender), and let it overlap or interlock where the reference does.
- The symbol's stroke weight should match the letter stems optically.
- Kern by hand: default spacing breaks at logo sizes. Tighter tracking needs a pair-by-pair spacing pass.
- Get optical balance on both light and dark backgrounds.
- Symbol and wordmark must also each work alone. For tiny sizes, use the symbol or a lifted letter, not the full lockup.

## 4. Process (UCDA 2, 3, 11)
- Sketch or explore many directions fast. Show only the strongest 2–3, each with a distinct emotion, never 5 near-identical fonts.
- Work in black and white first. Add colour only after the form is approved.
- Get a second look for unwanted shapes, hidden words, innuendo and cultural readings. Check every orientation and size.

## 5. Recall and scale (UCDA 5)
- One concept, a simple form, recognisable at a glance.
- Test at favicon size (16, 24, 32 px), at app-icon size and at billboard size. If it falls apart small, simplify or make a small-size variant.

## 6. Presentation (UCDA 7, 12, 13; board patterns)
- Present a system, not a single logo: primary lockup, symbol alone, wordmark alone, a monogram or lifted letter, a seal or badge, and how it breaks apart or repeats (Messy Studio sheet).
- One colour on a flat tone, with lots of air; the mark sits small on the page.
- Show it in context on real carriers: postcard, stamp, matchbook, billboard, app icon, site header, social post (Fieldman, Preserved, Calendly bento).
- Let the mark become graphics: a window or mask into imagery (Empee), stripes or paths extended from it (the billboard), patterns.
- Consider motion: how the mark animates.
- Wit or a human touch where the sector allows it.

## 7. Handover (UCDA 14)
- Usage guide: colour options, minimum and maximum sizes, clear space (exclusion zone), positioning, and don'ts (stretch, recolour, rotate, outline, effects).

## Checklist before showing
1. Is the emotion stated, and does each type choice trace to it?
2. Is there at least one custom detail, not a default font?
3. Does it match the reference's construction (measured, not eyeballed)?
4. B/W first? Readable at 16 px?
5. Does it break a competitor pattern?
6. Is it presented as a system, in context, on carriers?
7. Screenshot checked for collisions, overlaps and contrast at phone and desktop sizes.
