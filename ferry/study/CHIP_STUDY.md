### Dated or cheap
- Heavy blue header boarding pass with a plane arrow and drop shadow (example 2): looks like a 2015 template. Too many fields, no hierarchy between codes and gate.
- Glassy blurred gradient cards with white doodle route lines (example 4): reads as consumer toy and stock; blur plus gradient plus rotation all at once.
- Neon yellow pills on black with light rays (example 16): crypto cliche, the opposite of trust.
- Rotated, shadowed ticket piles with fake paper texture (examples 22, 26): fine as posters, but as UI they are costume; real UI never rotates.
- Rainbow tint status sets with a different hue per state (example 9) when the brand has one palette: reads generic, SaaS default.

## Patterns that repeat

1. Codes first, large, with a minimal connector. In every strong pass or widget (3, 22, 29, the island), the two codes are the biggest type and the connector is a plain line, arrow or small icon, about 1 px stroke or 1/10 of code height. Examples 1, 3, 22, 29.
2. Duration or progress is set on or under the route, not beside it. The island puts a dot on an arc, the YYZ widget puts a word under the line, the capsule tracker fills the line. Examples 3, 29, 31 (and FocusFlight from Mobbin).
3. Tear = a pair of half-circle notches plus either a dashed line or nothing. At small size the notches carry it; the dashed line is dropped or becomes a 1 px dotted rule. Examples 1, 27, 28, Meetup (Mobbin). Scallops only work where the tooth is 3 px or larger (26, 30).
4. Status is a pill of 18 to 24 px at 11 to 14 px type, tint fill, a distinct icon per state, and sits in a corner or under the midpoint. The simplest form is glyph plus word without a fill (11). Examples 9, 10, 11, 28, 29, 32.
5. Print ephemera over an image uses flat, opaque, hard-edged paper with one ink colour, not blur. The postmark overlaps the stamp by about a third and runs off its edge, in multiply. Examples 5, 17, 18, 19.
6. Painting plus UI works when the UI occupies the painting's empty zone and has a hairline border and a low shadow. Examples 33, 27 (painting as inset), 6 (painting as the whole face with a text strip).
7. Modern layer = mono small caps labels at 0.08 to 0.12em tracking, hairline ticks, a single glowing or accent marker, and tabular numerals. Examples 12, 13, 24, 31, FocusFlight, Legend (Mobbin).
8. One colour per object, with state shown by shape and a single accent. Metro passes, Suica, Andante, the Yodiz stamps (17, 21, 23).

## What this means for Ferry's chip

Proportions (my recommendation, by eye from the above):
- Overall ratio 3.2:1 to 3.6:1 wide, like the dynamic island (3.5:1), the capsule tracker (3:1) and the transit tickets (2.6:1 to 3:1). Standard size 288 x 84 px (3.4:1). Compact 176 x 40 px (4.4:1, only codes, route, tick). Hero 360 x 120 px (3:1).
- Radius: 10 to 12 px on the standard (about 13 percent of height), which sits between a pill and a card and fits a paper object. Compact radius 8 px. Do not use a full pill for the body; keep a full pill only for the inner status chip.
- Padding: 14 px inset on the standard (one sixth of height), 10 px on compact.
- Type: two sizes and two weights at most plus one label size. Codes 22 px Ferry Sans medium in the standard, 15 px in compact. Values 12 px regular. Labels 9 px mono caps at 0.1em tracking, 55 percent ink. Time in tabular mono 12 px.
- Layout: left body about 72 percent, stub about 28 percent. Stub holds status chip over time (two lines: chip 18 px high, time below at 11 px). Chip 18 px high, 10 px type, 6 px side padding, 11 px glyph leading.

Route line: code, then a 1 px line, then code. The line is drawn as a solid 1 px segment up to the current position and a dotted 1 px remainder (dots at 3 px pitch), with a small 6 px hull dot (or the stepped Ferry mark, scaled) at the current position, per the island and the capsule. Duration is set in the line's middle in 9 px mono with 6 px gaps (FocusFlight). On arrival the dot lands on the destination code and the line becomes solid.

Tear: two half-circle notches, radius 5 px (about 6 percent of height), cut out of the body at the stub boundary with the background showing through; between them a 1 px dotted rule at 3 px pitch, 40 percent opacity. No scallop on the 40 px size. Use scalloped (perforated) edges only on a stamp motif, not on the chip's body, and only when the tooth is at least 3 px.

At 40 px high (compact): drop labels, drop the stub; show FROM code, route line with position dot, TO code, and a 6 px state dot at the far right (glyph only like example 11: ring spinner, solid dot, check). Time appears only on tap or as a 9 px mono suffix after 120 px width. At 120 px high (hero): add small-caps labels over each code (9 px mono), chain names in the second line (12 px), duration in the line, the full stub with chip and time, and the postmark (a 56 px ring, 12 degrees rotation, multiply, ink in a warm dark blue) landing on the stub's top corner on arrival, overlapping the stub by about a third and bleeding 6 px past its edge, like example 17.

States: in flight = chip with a dashed-ring glyph, neutral tint; arrived = check glyph plus postmark; failed = x glyph. Same chip size in every state so nothing reflows. Status colour should come from the Ferry palette (not a rainbow): one accent for in-flight, ink for arrived, a warm red-brown for failed. Glyph shape differs by state so colour is not the only signal.

Modern layer on the painting: chip is opaque paper (warm off-white, 96 to 100 percent), 1 px border at 12 percent ink, shadow 0 1 2 at 8 percent plus 0 8 24 at 6 percent, no blur behind. Place it over the empty sky or water of the painting and overlap the subject by at most 10 percent (the ferry's wake may pass behind the chip's edge). The modern signal is the mono labels, the hairline ticks along the route (like example 12), tabular numerals and one live-moving dot; the paper edge and notches provide the classic read. Do not rotate the chip in product use; rotation only in print mockups.

## What to avoid
- Rainbow tint pills for every state, or colour as the only difference between states.
- Blurred glass over a painting, glowing neon, light rays, gradients on the chip body.
- Rotated and shadowed ticket piles in the UI itself (fine on posters).
- Barcodes as decoration (examples 1, 2): they say airline, not Ferry. If a stub needs a texture, use the stepped mark or a tick strip.
- Scalloped or heavily perforated edges on the body at chip size; teeth below 3 px go to mush.
- More than three type sizes, more than six fields; the island chip shows what is enough: two codes, one line, one time, one status.
- A plane or generic arrow icon between codes; use the route line with a position dot, or the Ferry hull.
- Covering the painting's subject with the chip.

### Corrections against Ferry's rules
- No mono typeface. Ferry is Ferry Sans only: labels are Ferry Sans Bold caps, tracked, with tabular figures. No "medium" weight exists; use Regular and Bold.
- The tear uses Ferry's stepped notch from the stamp, not a half circle. The study's lesson still holds: features under 3 px turn to mush, so the notch is simplified or dropped at 40 px.
- The incident colour is Signal, once per view. No second red.

### A. Live cross-chain transfers (public explorers)

1. LI.FI Scan (scan.li.fi). 4. Relay transactions (relay.link/transactions). Light UI, Live dot (green, 6 px) on the filter bar. Row about 50 px high, columns From, To, Transactions, Status, Fill Time. ### B. Brand identities that turn routes, tickets and status into graphics
Source for this section is text (case studies and trade press); the studio pages were read as text, not viewed, so visual detail is limited to what the writing states. Image-level construction is (inferred) unless noted.

1. Flighty (flighty.com, teardown by Blake Crosley and Apple Behind the Design coverage). Built on the airport departure board: one line per flight, columns flight, destination, gate, time, status. Four status colours only (green on time, amber delayed, red cancelled, blue boarding) on a capsule badge in bold caption type, white text on the status colour. Type scale in the teardown: 32 countdown, 24 gate, 20 flight number, 18 time, 16 body, 14 status, 12 label, so seven sizes but each tied to one role, and numbers are tabular. The route is a 3 pt line, 8 pt end dots, 40 pt inset, 60 pt frame height, a marker at the current position, "Departed 45m ago" at left and "2h 15m remaining" at right. A compact form of the same data is a circular arc filling clockwise. Progress moves linearly over 60 seconds, slow and continuous, not springy. The big idea is the 15 named states (far out, day before, head to airport, at gate, boarding, on board, taxiing, in flight, descending, landed, connection, completed, delayed): the same card shows different content per state, which is the model for Ferry's states.
2. Wise rebrand (Ragged Edge, 2023; the-brandidentity.com interview). Two typefaces only: a custom headline face (Wise Sans, drawn from Parafina, about 150 added glyphs and every currency symbol) and a customised Inter for product. Colour tested with APCA, not only WCAG. Icons are linear strokes on a grid with equal positive and negative space, deliberately not chunky. The brand graphic is the "tapestry": photographs and textures woven from ten real customer routes plotted on the globe, built to feel in motion when still. The article gives nothing about transfer status visuals; status in the app is the plain chip plus a vertical timeline (first pass). Lesson: the route is the brand image (data from real routes becomes the pattern), and product type is a quiet workhorse next to a loud headline face.
3. Eurostar (DesignStudio, now Further, 2023; Creative Boom). The spark mark replaced the metal ribbon "e" and works as a north star reaching "toward destinations"; it appears on livery, in stations and on screens. Wordmark in lowercase italic bespoke type (Or Type) quoting the 1994 logo. Deep navy and a punchy blue, six secondary colours drawn from the continent. Destination artworks by seven illustrators in five countries (a Cologne Cathedral illustration with a fare line "London to Cologne from" on an out-of-home poster): the painted city carries the place, and the fare line is a single plain sentence. Nothing published on ticket or route graphics. Lesson: place goes to illustration, route and price go to one line of plain type.
4. PostNL (Studio Dumbar and DEPT). A coloured "post-line" motif drawn from the outline of the logo, used on digital, motion, print, uniforms, liveries, mailboxes and kiosks, while the logo itself stays strictly orange and white with no gradient. The stated mechanism is that the motif is cut from the mark's own contour. Parcel tracking use is not described. Lesson: one line cut from the mark's own shape, in colour, as the connective device across touchpoints, while the logo stays one flat colour. For Ferry the same job would be done by the stepped notch run as a line.
5. Trainline (Studio Blackburn 2017, WK Amsterdam "The Way To Train" later). The "t" is drawn as a crossing of routes; mint plus a mobile-first typeface. Reviewers called the 2017 system disjointed at small sizes. The later platform repositions the brand from selling tickets to the journey itself. Dated lesson: a letter built from a crossing does not survive 16 px.
6. Tempo (payments chain identity, June 2026, Design Compass). Wordmark is stretched by custom software from normal to very wide forms as a metaphor for cross-border payment flow; a standalone T for avatars; motion and sound built in. Restrained palette. No status visuals described. Lesson: a payments brand can make the wordmark itself the route (width as distance). Ferry's wordmark could carry a stretch state for in-flight (inferred, not suggested for Ferry's fixed rules).
7. Worldpay (identity, search summary only): logo drawn from the data trails money leaves as it travels, typeface based on the same paths. Only a summary was read, treat as (inferred).

Not reached: Brightline (no result), SNCF Connect, Amtrak, An Post, Royal Mail, Citymapper; brand-identity sites gave only the Wise interview and a newsletter stub. BP&O and Brand New articles were not found by search.

### C. Live sites with product UI over imagery
Godly and Awwwards searches again returned only listing and tag pages, so four live sites were opened directly (guessed, not curated). None is gouache; they are photo or render heroes. The two that matter show opposite ways to lay UI on an image.

1. Mercury (mercury.com). Full-bleed hero, one landscape (misty valley at dusk, warm pink sky, grass foreground) with a desk, chair and laptop placed in it. The headline and email field sit in the sky (top 30 percent, white type about 26 px centred, 14 px subline), and the product UI is not a card at all: it is the laptop screen, a literal object in the scene at about 15 percent of the width, dark UI on a dark screen. A dark gradient scrim runs across the top for the nav and footer line only. On scroll the headline fades out and the laptop is left alone. Lesson for Ferry: the UI can be an object in the painting (a screen, a ticket on a desk), so no card has to cover paint. The heavy photo realism and vignette are the dated part (it reads as an AI render).
2. Monarch Money (monarch.com). Photo hero (woman with phone) with two frosted glass cards "Flex Spending" and "Fixed" floating at the left and right edges, partly overlapping the subject's shoulder, about 150 x 80 px, 50 percent grey-white blur, white 14 px bold title and 11 px label with amount at 16 px; a plain frosted panel at the bottom left holds the headline (serif, about 20 px) and an orange pill button. An orange swoosh line crosses the photo. Cards overlap the person; two sizes of card and no border. This is exactly the blur-over-image look the first pass warned against; it also covers the subject. Useful only as a counter-example.
3. Wealthsimple (wealthsimple.com). Navy field with one 3D rendered gold coin carrying the W, headline left in a geometric sans at about 36 px, white pill button; below the hero the page flips to warm off-white with a very large serif product list (Chequing, Trade, Invest) centred at about 70 px. No UI over the image at all: product UI is held back to the sections below. Floating nav is a white rounded bar (radius about 16 px) with a thin banner above it, both inset 8 px from the edge. Lesson: the dark hero object and the cream product sections are two separate worlds, joined by a hard horizontal edge.
4. Rainbow (rainbow.me). Dark page, a real phone frame left (about 220 px wide) with the live app (profile, four round blue actions, lists), copy right in two greys, a QR code tile bottom right, a section nav at the right edge in small grey (Home, Crypto, Predictions, Perps, Live Data, Rewards). No image behind it. The product is the phone and the scroll sticks it while sections change beside it. Lesson: a fixed phone with sections changing beside it works as a status demonstrator, but there is no painting. Included for the sticky-device pattern only.

Not reached: any site with painted or illustrated hero plus product card (searches returned thumbnails and tag pages only). Legend from the first pass remains the nearest painted-style precedent.

### New patterns (not in the first pass)

1. Status as a time-ordered stepper with a duration afterwards. Axelarscan: five 20 px discs on a 2 px line, 7 px captions, "32s" with a clock glyph after completion. Relay: a single full-width 4 px bar on the detail page, "1s" fill time as its own column. Both show the duration only once complete.
2. Named states drive different content in one component. Flighty has about 15 states and the card swaps what it shows per state; the Dynamic Island compact form shows a different pair of facts again. A chip needs a state table (what is shown in each), not one layout.
3. Progress speed is part of the design. Flighty moves its progress marker linearly over 60 seconds, slow and continuous, no spring. A live chip should creep, not bounce.
4. Brand line cut from the mark. PostNL's post-line derives from the logo outline; Tempo's wordmark stretches. The connective device across vans, posters and UI is a line made from the mark itself.
5. Real data as pattern. Wise's tapestries are woven from ten actual customer routes. Ferry's real routes (from chain, to chain) could draw its own pattern.
6. UI as an object inside the scene. Mercury's laptop screen is the product UI, placed in the painted landscape, so nothing covers the paint.
7. Chain as a corner badge on the asset icon, with the chain name never spelled out in lists (LI.FI, Relay, Across). Chain names are spelled out only on detail pages (Axelarscan path, WormholeScan chains).
8. Verb choice: "Fulfilled" (deBridge), "Finalized" (Wormhole), "Received" and "Executed" (Axelar), "Success" (Relay). Words are small and caps or sentence case; none of the five says "Delivered" or "Arrived".
9. Live indicator: a 6 px green dot beside "Live" or a toggle that streams new rows (Relay, Wormhole). The dot says the list is moving.

### Changes this suggests for the chip

- Write a state table before drawing: for each of Queued, Departed, In transit, Arrived, Delayed, Failed say which fields show (for example in transit: codes, line with position dot, time elapsed; arrived: codes, line solid, duration once, postmark). Flighty shows each state needs its own content, not one layout with a changed label.
- Show duration only when it means something: while in flight, elapsed time counts quietly (set in Ferry Sans Bold caps with tabular figures, label side of the line); on arrival it is replaced by the final duration with a small clock glyph, as Axelarscan and Relay do. The one number that sets Ferry apart (arrival time in seconds) gets its own slot, not a place inside a label.
- Offer a stepper form for the hero chip: 4 to 5 nodes on the route line with 7 to 8 px captions (Sent, Confirmed, Landed) and the solid line to the last passed node, matching Axelarscan. Keep the compact chip to a single line with one dot.
- Chain on the chip as a corner badge on the code or on a small hull mark, spelled out only in the hero size and in the detail view (LI.FI, Relay, Across pattern).
- Slow the dot: linear, continuous, about 60 s from end to end for display demos (Flighty), never a spring. Status text changes by cross-fade only.
- Colour: keep Signal once per view. In flight can use ink with Signal on the position dot; arrived goes to ink with the postmark; failure goes to Signal and replaces the dot, so a view with a failure has no other Signal. Four status colours (Flighty) is too many for Ferry; the glyph and the word carry state.
- Over the painting: do not cover paint. Either place the chip in an empty zone with an opaque paper face and a hairline (first pass), or make the chip an object in the scene (a ticket on a table, a board on the quay), as Mercury places its UI as a laptop screen. Avoid frosted cards that overlap the subject (Monarch).
- Brand device across touchpoints: draw the route line from the stepped notch (PostNL logic) and use it on the chip, the postcard, the ticket edge and on posters, so the product chip and the print share one line.
- Pattern idea for mockups that need recognition: a field of real routes plotted as stepped lines on a painted map, in the manner of Wise tapestries (inferred, not seen as a visual).
