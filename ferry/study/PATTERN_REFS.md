# Pattern references for Ferry

Four Pinterest pins studied from the page itself (frames stepped by hand where there was motion). Anything not clearly seen is marked (inferred). Three of the four are motion; the fourth is a carousel that teaches how motion becomes pattern. None of the four shows type set over a pattern in a full poster, so the "use" notes are mostly about how the pattern fills a frame.

## Pin 1. "Fifth Row patterns" (Ashfall Studio, pinned by Vinay Sateesh)

Source link: none shown. Title and "By Ashfall Studio" only. A 15 second video, tall portrait frame.

Unit shapes: one. A long thin horizontal bar, stacked with a gap of about the bar's own height into a ruled field of rows (about 30 rows). The bars have softly rounded ends. There is no second shape; everything else is the bar changing length.

Rule: the field is split into five vertical columns of equal width. Each row's bar is cut at column boundaries, so a bar is a run of one to five column cells. Row length varies smoothly from row to row, which makes a diagonal or bell shaped edge. The wave of lengths moves: mid-video the orange runs bulge to a tall central block with a violet notch tapering top and bottom; later the orange pushes to the left half and violet takes the right, with the edge stepping one column per row in a staircase. Early on the frame is almost all violet with thin orange hairlines at the row gaps, so rows are always present as a ground and the "figure" is the other colour filling them.

Recognisable because: the ruled horizontal grain never goes away, and the stepped edge between two colours is always on a five-column grid.

Colour: two flat saturated colours (violet and orange), swapping figure and ground by run length, a dim brown wash at the bottom edge (a gradient overlay; Ferry should not copy that). A pink seam appears where colours meet.

Motion, frames at 0.5, 3, 6, 9, 13 s: at 0.5 s all violet with orange hairlines. At 3 s orange has grown from the bottom-left as short bars, still sparse. At 6 s orange has flooded the field with a violet vertical lozenge in the centre, widest at mid height. At 9 s the violet recedes to the far left and right columns, orange owns the centre three. At 13 s the edge is a descending staircase, orange left and violet right. Bars grow and shrink in length by whole column steps with soft easing, staggered row by row so the change travels down the frame. Looks like a slow loop, about 15 s (inferred loop).

Use: full ground, edge to edge, no type shown. It would take 100 percent of a frame.

## Pin 2. "Studio Lafo -> Visual System Animation" (fffunction.studio)

Source link: a "Visit site" button is present, target not read. Creator fffunction.studio, pinned to a board called "37 - website". 7 second looping video, square.

Unit shapes: one tall vertical bar that is notched in steps near its top, so each bar reads as a tapering tower: wide shaft with a short stepped taper, ending in a hairline. Bars stand shoulder to shoulder at equal spacing along the bottom edge.

Rule: bars are repeated across the full width at a constant pitch. Height and taper vary progressively along the row: the left bars are thick and tall, the bars to the right get thinner and shorter, so the row reads as a perspective fan or a descending sweep. The ground flips between pale lime and black, and the bars flip to the opposite colour (inverse).

Recognisable because: stepped tapers that all point up and shrink in a fixed ratio across the row; the look of a sound meter or organ pipes.

Colour: two colours only, near black and pale lime-cream (about F0FBC8, inferred), swapped as figure and ground over time.

Motion, frames at 0.2, 1.2, 2.4, 3.6, 5.0 s: at 0.2 s pale ground with hairlines faintly starting bottom left. At 1.2 s black towers fill the left and centre, tapering to pale on the right, each tower stepped on both sides. At 2.4 s the field is entirely black (the colour flip, the towers merged). At 3.6 s pale towers rise out of black from bottom left, mirrored version of the 1.2 s frame. At 5.0 s the field is pale again with hairlines at top right, retreating. So the sequence is: lines appear, grow into towers, merge to solid, invert, shrink to lines. Bars grow with a staggered delay left to right. The loop closes at the pale blank state.

Use: full frame ground, very bold, no type shown. Likely used as a title or ident background (inferred).

## Pin 3. Visual System pin, board "devx" (Visual System, pinned "Pin on Brand design")

Source link: a "Visit site" button, target not read. Creator shown as "Visual System". No title. A video of about 8 seconds, portrait.

Unit shapes: one vertical rectangle in two colours. The frame is divided into four horizontal bands. In each band the same unit repeats at a different width: the bottom band has 5 wide bars, the next band up has about 8, then about 13, then about 20 and thinner at the top. It is the same unit scaled in steps (a ratio of roughly 1.6 each band, inferred).

Rule: each band offsets the bar pattern half a step against the band above, so the bars interlock like piano keys: black keys and lime keys, long and short, alternating. Bands are separated by a hard horizontal line. The sequence at 0.3 s shows the bars at random widths and mismatched; by 2.2 to 3.4 s the bars have slid into regular rhythm where each band is a clean alternation. Within a band the bars grow and shrink in width and the lime fills the black gaps, so the state change is bar width, not colour.

Recognisable because: the piano keyboard rhythm, with a hard step in scale between bands.

Colour: two flat colours, acid lime and near black, plus a soft darkening to olive at the very bottom (a gradient; avoid).

Motion, frames at 0.3, 1.2, 2.2, 3.4, 4.6 s: at 0.3 s the bars are irregular, widths differ in every band, small stray squares floating (tiny notches). At 1.2 s the top band has already settled to thin regular stripes while the lower bands are still irregular. At 2.2 s the top two bands are regular, the lower two settling. At 3.4 s all four bands are a clean alternating pattern at their own scale. At 4.6 s it breaks up again into big irregular blocks and the top band gets wide blocks. So the ordering is: top band settles first, then each lower band; resolved state holds briefly, then loosens. Looks like a loop (inferred).

Use: full ground edge to edge. The two-colour contrast is high enough that type would need a quiet panel or a knock-out. Not shown with type.

## Pin 4. "Pattern animations" carousel (studio.baseline, pinned on a board named "archivean")

Source link: "Visit site" present, target not read. Creator studio.baseline (Instagram handle shown as @studio.baseline). A 9-slide carousel, each slide a 3 to 7 second video, tall portrait. Caption: "From one tiny movement to an endless pattern. Pattern animations always look complex. But they're often simpler than they seem. In this carousel, I'm breaking down the process step by step showing you which factors you can optimise and play with to transform a simple animation into a creative, seamless pattern." This is the only pin that states the method.

Slides seen: (1) cover, vertical blue bars on black with the line "Pattern animations always look complex. But they're often simpler than they seem." (3 s). (3) "Create one tiny animation: a short, simple movement is all you need as your base unit." One blue upright bar, centred, on pale grey, on a pale ground. (4) "Duplicate it and line them up: copy your animation and line them up in a row, a grid, or offset them." A row of ten identical bars with equal gaps, bleeding off both sides. (5) "Play around with timing: stagger all the copies or vary the easing and speed." Bars here are thin, mostly invisible with one or two lit, so a wave passes along the row. (6) "Zooming out: take your units and multiply it again. This creates a bigger, complex-looking pattern." The frame stayed blank grey in my captures, so I could not see the zoomed-out result. (7) "Change arrangement: now add variation like rotation or mirroring. It creates a completely different rhythm." Thin vertical lines in four blocks arranged in a stepped layout, mirrored and shifted, with line weights fading from bold to hairline. Slides 2, 8 and 9 were not opened.

Unit: one blue bar (a tall rectangle, about 1 to 4.5 ratio).

Rule: five steps. One animated unit, copied into a row, staggered in time, repeated again as blocks, then rotated and mirrored. The pattern is the same unit at every level; only copy count, offset, timing and orientation change.

Recognisable because: a single stroke that is repeated rhythmically; the unit is always the same, so the eye reads it as one thing.

Colour: one saturated blue on pale grey or black. One accent, two grounds.

Motion: the unit does a small move (grows or slides along its length), the copies start at staggered offsets, so a wave runs along the row. Soft easing. I did not see the final loop.

Use: pattern as a full-bleed ground on the cover, as a band in the middle of the slide behind a text line, and as a field on the pale slides with the headline pinned bottom left in a light grotesque and two lines of small text under it.

## What the four share

1. One unit, always. Every pattern is a single shape (bar, stepped tower, key) repeated; richness comes from count, scale and offset, not from new shapes.
2. A fixed grid with a progression. Column grids (pin 1), bands in a scale ratio (pin 3), pitch with graded size (pin 2), grid or offset rows (pin 4). The pattern always has a simple underlying grid.
3. Two colours, figure and ground that can swap. Nothing uses more than two colours at once; inversion is the main motion event.
4. Motion is staggered, not simultaneous. Every piece starts a little after its neighbour, so a wave crosses the frame and the final pattern emerges from the wave.
5. A resolved state that holds. The pattern is most recognisable at the moment all units snap into rhythm, and then loosens; the loop passes through order.
6. The pattern is the whole ground. All four fill the frame edge to edge and are cropped by it; none uses a small tile or a frame with a margin.

## A recipe for Ferry

Cut from the two stepped shores that face each other across a gap. Take one shore (a stepped block: a tall step and a lower step) and treat the gap as a real part of the system.

Unit shapes (4):
- Tall step: the high block of a shore, a plain rectangle.
- Low step: the lower block of the same shore, half the height or width of the tall step.
- Gap: a narrow negative bar, the channel between two shores. It is always drawn as paper, not as an ink shape, and has fixed width one quarter of a step.
- Mirror shore: the same pair flipped, so two shores face each other.
Optional fifth: Hairline, a thin line the width of the gap, used to carry a wave and sit in the gap.

Assembly rules:
- Base grid: columns of one step; rows of one step. All shapes sit on it.
- Shores sit as pairs left and right of a gap, tall step out, low step in, so they interlock like the logo.
- Rows repeat the pair with a half-step offset in alternate rows (as pin 3).
- Scale steps: bands may use the unit at x1, x1.5 and x2 (a restrained ratio; no more than three scales).
- Avoid stepped zigzag diagonals; steps run in at most two heights so it reads as shores, not as a stepwell or carved screen. Keep edges straight; steps are a single riser, not a staircase.
- Colour: two colours per piece, for example Ink on Paper, Harbour on Mist, Sea glass on Harbour; a single Stone or Sea grass accent only on the gap or one shore. No gradients.

Motion behaviours (calm, no bounce, ease in-out, 4 to 8 s loops):
1. Close the gap. Shores slide in from the sides, staggered by row, and meet so the gap narrows to the fixed width; the hold is the resolved pattern, then the gap widens slowly again.
2. Tide. A wave of length change passes down the rows as in pin 1: each row's tall step grows or shrinks by one grid step, staggered, so the stepped edge drifts.
3. Swap. Figure and ground swap with a slow wipe across the frame (as pin 2), shores becoming the gaps and the gaps becoming shores, passing through a solid colour.

Controls for a generator tool:
- Palette: pick two from the seven brand colours, plus swap.
- Grid: columns (3 to 12), rows, unit size, gap width.
- Shapes: tall step ratio, low step ratio, include hairline, mirror on or off.
- Offset: alternate row offset (0, half, quarter).
- Scale bands: count (1 to 3) and ratio.
- Crop: scale and position of the pattern in the frame; edge bleed on or off.
- Motion: behaviour (close the gap, tide, swap), duration, stagger amount, easing, loop on or off, seed for the stray variation.
- Export: still PNG or SVG at poster sizes (4:5 and 16:9), looping MP4 or GIF, plus a transparent version for use as a mask over images, and a safe-area overlay showing where type can sit.
