# Ferry: gap analysis

Nov 12, 2026. Director's read, from three inputs: `CALENDLY_STUDY.md` (the 2026 Smith & Diction rebrand), `BRAND_AUDIT.md` (what exists on disk), and the six 4:5 posters from the Swiss grid. The X presentation study is still with the mockup fork and will be folded in when it reports.

## The short version

Ferry has good parts and no system. The logo, Ferry Sans, the palette, the paintings, the stamp and the animations are each well made. Nothing ties them together except the paper background. A finished brand has one idea and one shape behaviour that show up in every piece. Ferry has the idea and has not yet chosen the shape.

## Where it falls short

### 1. No formal primitive
Calendly has one concept ("make space in your day") and one form (the ripple, with its soft "gloop" joins). That form is in the logo, icons, containers, loader, motion and bento tiles.

Ferry's concept is clear: every transfer is a crossing you can see. But its devices come from five different places: stamp perforations, a knife edge, embossing, the watercolour bleed and a Swiss grid. None of them comes from the mark. The mark itself holds the obvious primitive: two stepped shores with a channel between them. That step and that gap should set the container shape, the crops, the icon style, the loader and the motion.

### 2. No product truth in the graphics
Calendly's strongest move is using real product data as the headline: a chip reading [icon][Fitness Session][Scheduled], or "11:30" at display size. It works as both a billboard and UI.

Ferry's only product moment is "41s" on the stamp and one poster. There is no transfer chip, no route, no status, no amount. For a payments brand the receipt is the most-seen surface, and Ferry has none. Ferry needs its own elastic container, the equivalent of the flex bubble: a ticket-like chip that holds from, to, status and time, and stretches to any copy length.

### 3. One type voice
All six posters are Ferry Sans Bold in one ink on cream. The italic is the only variation. This is the main reason they do not feel modern.

Calendly pairs a sans set-up with a serif payoff inside one headline, and sets its numbers in the serif at display size. Ferry already has the ingredients: Ferry Sans, a serif used once for "41s", and a script for place names. They are not yet used together, and the written rules disagree about whether headlines are serif or sans.

### 4. Why the posters read as dated
Seen on the six posters:
- The painting is always a rectangle placed on the grid. Type and image never touch, overlap or crop each other.
- Every poster sits on the same cream paper. There is no colour ground, no painted ground and no dark poster.
- Type sizes stay in a narrow, polite range. Nothing is cropped by the frame and nothing is very large or very small.
- The logo is always small at bottom left. It is never split, centred or used as structure.
- There is no mid-ground layer. Calendly puts a pattern between background and subject for depth.
- Poster 1, the mark as a window onto the rowboats, is the strongest. It is the only one where the brand's shape does the layout work.

The result is tasteful Swiss print from a past decade: correct, calm and anonymous.

### 5. Colour is paper plus ink
The palette page exists, but in use nearly everything is ink on cream. Calendly treats colour as an environment: a small core, accent triplets and a named category of gradients drawn from real light. Ferry's equivalent is already in hand: blurred or cropped gouache as the colour field. The paintings should supply the grounds, not just sit in boxes.

### 6. Missing touchpoints
Nothing exists yet for: product UI, landing page, app icon in context, X header and post templates, link-preview image, motion ident, transfer receipt and email, status states, route maps, tickets, pitch deck or brand sheet. Calendly's own case study skipped email, deck, social kit and print, so Ferry's print work is a real advantage to keep.

### 7. Presentation
Ferry's pieces are shown one at a time on neutral grounds. Calendly opens on a bento of the whole system, shows concept, construction, iteration and application for each part, animates half its figures, and closes on a second bento that contains the first. Ferry has no summary sheet and no sequence.

### 8. Foundations that block shipping
- Every painting is made from a Pinterest photo with no recorded source or licence. Nothing can be published until Ferry has paintings from sources it owns.
- Nothing in `ferry/` has ever been committed, and 130 MB of work sits only in a git-ignored folder.
- The forks' rules contradict each other (see rulings below).

## Proposed rulings on the contradictions

| Topic | Ruling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Headline type | **Client ruling, Nov 12:** Ferry Sans in its watercolour (bleed) version for headlines; plain Ferry Sans for body, UI and everything else. The handwritten face is for postcards only and the cursive for stamps only, both rare | Ferry Sans is the brand's own face; the bleed version is what makes a headline Ferry's and nobody else's. This replaces the earlier sans-plus-serif proposal |
| Serif | **Client ruling, Nov 12:** Ferry Sans only. No serif anywhere except the stamp value already approved | One brand face keeps the identity Ferry's own |
| Colour and environment | **Client ruling, Nov 12:** the paintings become the environment (grounds, crops, blurred or enlarged passages). No abstract gradients; they do not suit Ferry. Draw ideas from the social and mockup study | The brand's own medium supplies the colour world |
| Medium | Gouache | Everything approved is gouache; the brief is out of date |
| Grid | Keep the 10 px Swiss grid for alignment; containers and crops come from the mark | Grid for order, mark for character |
| Formats | 4:5 and 16:9 as masters; X header, link preview and 9:16 derived only when a touchpoint needs them | Matches the latest decision |
| Bleed | Paintings may bleed off the frame; type may be cropped by it on purpose | The "nothing bleeds" rule is already broken by its own layouts |
| App icon | Harbour ground with the Paper mark; re-export and retire the bright wash | The rule exists; the file is stale |

## Build order

1. **Primitive and transfer chip.** Define the step-and-channel shape language and the ticket chip, with an anatomy sheet. Everything else depends on this.
2. **Type system.** Choose the serif, write the pairing rule, and set numerals as display type.
3. **Posters, second pass.** Rebuild on 1 and 2, using the typography references still to come.
4. **Colour as environment.** Blurred gouache grounds and a pattern or mask layer cut from the mark.
5. **Product surfaces.** Transfer status screen, receipt, app icon in context.
6. **Landing hero and X kit.**
7. **Motion ident and summary bento.**

In parallel, as housekeeping: source paintings Ferry owns, commit the work, and fix the stale files.

## Client feedback on the first mockups and social assets (Nov 12)

The mockup fork's first assets were rejected: empty space, a clean colour field and a poster on top. The study behind them (`MOCKUP_PRESENTATION.md`) shows work full of pattern, ornament, heritage material and clear brand recognition.

Director's read of why:
- The study's "branded stage" rule says "a field of one brand colour" with the logo, tagline and "the brand's ornament in the corners". The brands studied own ornament and material (engravings, gold relief, halftone, generative patterns). Ferry owns none, so its stage is only the colour field.
- This is gap 1 again: nothing derives from the mark, so there is no pattern, ornament or device to fill a frame with.
- The posters used as the hero are the ones already judged not modern enough.

Consequences:
- Mockups and social assets wait until the base exists: shape language, a pattern or texture layer from the mark and the paintings, and reworked posters.
- Add a test to every presentation brief: cover the logo, and the piece must still be recognisably Ferry.
- No empty flat-colour grounds. The frame is filled by Ferry's own pattern, painting or material.
- Present sets (four-up, flipbook, series), never one lonely asset.
