# Calendly 2026 rebrand: a principles study

Study date: 2026-11-12. Written to be transferable to another brand (Ferry, a cross-chain payments brand with gouache imagery), so every section ends in what the move does, not only what it looks like.

## 1. The idea

**"Calendly is the easiest way to make space in your day."** The studio's working concept was *making space*: space on the calendar for the conversations that matter, and space in the day because Calendly does the back-and-forth.

There is a second, visual idea: **the ripple**. One link sent sets off a widening ring of consequences (a meeting, a client, a business). In the case study's words, "Calendly is that first ripple."

Making space is the *feeling*. The ripple is the *form*. Everything traces back to one or both:

| Part | Traces to | How |
|---|---|---|
| Symbol | Ripple | A C inside one asymmetric ring, inside a squircle: three concentric "waves" |
| Wordmark | Connection (the ripple's job) | Tight tracking made letters touch. The C tail flows into the a and the e is tilted into the n ("invisible connections") |
| "Gloop" | Ripple as liquid | The soft neck where two forms meet, like water drops merging. It is used inside icons and between bubbles |
| Flex bubbles | Space plus connection | Rounded containers that hold one piece of meeting data each (icon, event, status, time), joined by a gloop bridge |
| Colour | Space, calm | Cool blue (the hyperlink heritage) plus pastel "environmental gradients" from light on desks, sunset cloud edges and turquoise water |
| Imagery | Space | Blurred, dreamy colour fields with no hard edges, and people shown mid-life, not mid-meeting (tennis, running, surfing, a makeup artist) |
| Pattern | Time | Vector stripes and blocks that read as "time blocks" on a calendar. Each pattern has a named mood |
| Type | Calm confidence plus warmth | A sharp-yet-round sans (TT Neoris) for structure, a soft serif (Wulkan Text) with "gloop" for warmth and for time |
| Motion | Ripple, flex | Rings expanding, bubbles gloop-joining, patterns drifting slowly behind the subject |

**Principle:** one concept phrase, one formal primitive. The concept says what the brand *gives* people, not what the product *does*. The client asked them not to draw calendars ("you just want to draw calendars"). The primitive carries the concept into every asset.

## 2. The system's parts

### 2.1 Logo
- **Symbol:** a C inside a single asymmetric ripple, inside a squircle (a shape between circle and rounded square). The ring is deliberately wobbly ("perfectly wobbly"). A 12-up iteration board shows the search: more rings, rounder or squarer outer shapes, fewer counters. An exploration board shows rejected routes (a radial burst C, a triple-line C, a blocky C in a squircle, a perspective "AC").
- **Construction:** a navy construction sheet shows the symbol as three nested squircle contours with Bézier handles drawn in. The outer shape is near-square with very soft corners. The C is a geometric C with a squared terminal.
- **App icon:** the same symbol rendered as a glossy, slightly embossed iOS-style icon. It is cornflower with a white ring, inner glow and a subtle purple edge tint. It is shown on an iPhone home screen, a desktop app sidebar, and as the centre tile of the hero bento.
- **Flat variants seen:** navy on gradient, white knock-out on photo and gradient, cornflower on pale lime, a pencil-sketch version on paper (an "origin" moment in the GIF), a navy line version on the app chrome, and a small white symbol top-centre on ads.
- **Wordmark:** a custom bold geometric sans in Midnight navy. Construction is shown as an overlay: outline points on a blue band, filled marks on grey, with grid lines through the stems. Highlighted connector segments show the C to a and e to n joins. It is heavier and tighter than the 2021 wordmark, and the "y" matches TT Neoris's y.
- **Lockups:** symbol left of wordmark, horizontal (before/after board). On ads the symbol sits top-centre and the wordmark bottom-centre, **split**, not locked. This is the most-used ad lockup in the set.
- **Sub-brand marks:** five product icons (Scheduling hourglass, Notetaker quill/leaf, Contacts figure, Callie four-point star, Payments card). They share stroke weight, rounded terminals, internal "gloop" joins, and an annotated anatomy sheet ("Varying line weight to mirror logo geometry", "Soft rounded corners with no sharp edges", "Shared geometry for unity across the suite", "Strong sense of symmetry and balance", "Tangible concepts for instant recognizability"). In product each icon sits in the same squircle as the master mark and is colour coded.

### 2.2 Colour (read off the case study's palette slide)
- **Core:** White #FFFFFF, Magnolia #FCFBF8 (warm off-white, the real page colour), Rain #E5EFFB, Sky #9ECCFF, **Cornflower #6AB0FF** (the hero blue), Lake #1D5DA5, **Midnight #081D35** (text, wordmark, dark grounds).
- **Product accents in tint/mid/dark triplets:** New Leaf #E7F0C9 / Lime #D4E892 / Avocado #536124; Lavender #ECE6FA / Lilac #B89EFA / Plum #473082; Aquamarine #D6F5F3 / Jade #23CDC2 / Emerald #0B5E60; Sunbeam #FCF1DE / Tangerine #FE9B23 / Coffee #5B3D20.
- **"Environmental gradients":** a named colour *category*, not a swatch. These are soft peach to pink to lilac to blue to teal fields with grain. Colour cards show each swatch as a small white label chip (name + hex) floating on a gradient card. The colour is introduced *inside its environment*, not as a flat chip.
- **Proportion** *(inferred from the palette build animation)*: the bands are sized by use. White is huge, Cornflower large, Midnight medium, then the gradient band, then the thin accent rows. The slide doubles as a usage-ratio chart.
- **Why the change:** the 2021 neon pink "isn't printable" and the blue barely printed. The new palette was chosen to survive print and large surfaces without shouting.

### 2.3 Typography
- **TT Neoris** (TypeType): primary. H1s, headlines, UI headings. Chosen because it is "approachable" but has sharp moments (K) and round ones (a, t), and its lowercase y matches the wordmark ("it's not Inter").
- **Wulkan Text**: support serif with "the right amount of gloop" to echo the icons. Specimen note: "Use only for titles, headlines, and large brand callouts."
- **Geist** (Vercel): product body and small text, "used primarily for product and website text under 18px".
- **How type is played with (seen):**
  - **Sans then serif in one headline.** Line 1 in Neoris, line 2 in Wulkan: "No easier way / to find time.", "The tools you rely on, / time and time again.", "Chasing a new PR is hard. / Scheduling sessions is easy." The serif line is always the emotional payoff. The sans line is always the set-up.
  - **Serif numerals as graphics.** Times in Wulkan at display size: "11:30" and "11:45" in two flex bubbles joined by a gloop bridge, with "Start" and "End" labels. "6:25 Run" is cropped at the tile edge in the bento. "6:30", "9:00", "2:30" and "8:00" appear on blurred-photo event cards. Time is *the* brand content, so numerals become the hero type.
  - **Status words in serif:** in a flex-bubble chain [icon][Event type in Neoris][Status in Wulkan], for example [hourglass][Patient Visit][Scheduled]. The serif marks the state change.
  - **Cropping at extreme scale:** the flex-bubble anatomy poster crops "sit | Sc" as outlined letterforms inside outlined bubbles, labelled "Detail" and "Bridge". Specimen covers set the face names huge between guide lines on navy.
  - **Type on image:** white Neoris/Wulkan straight on photography (tennis ad). Or type inside a white flex bubble on a gradient or photo, which keeps legibility without a scrim.
  - **Editorial micro-labels:** small caps tracked labels ("THE CALENDLY PLATFORM", "SCHEDULING"), italic serif figure labels ("fig 01.") and "1 OF 12" counters on presentation boards.

### 2.4 Imagery and photography
- **Photography:** warm, film-like, slightly grainy, real people in leisure and craft moments: a tennis player shot from below against the sky, a runner stretching, friends with surfboards, a makeup artist's face, a hand holding a red card over a laptop. Mostly low angle or close crop with plenty of sky or space. Subjects look away from the camera and are often laughing.
- **Generated colour fields:** at Calendly's request the team used Midjourney (Gavin Potenza). The output was demoted to **backgrounds and colour references only**, a 12-up grid of blurred, motion-smeared landscapes "like if you tried to paint what a breeze might look like." Blurred workspace scenes appear inside event cards ("Yoga session with Caroline 6:30").
- **Blur is a system device:** blurred photos sit under the time cards and inside product mockups, and blurred fields are what Patternly slices.

### 2.5 Illustration
There is effectively **none** in the figurative sense, which is a deliberate reversal of 2021's layered shapes. The "illustration" work is done by gradients, patterns and the icon set.

### 2.6 Iconography
Covered in 2.1. Notable presentation moves: the icons are shown vertically, each in a frosted glass squircle on a blurred field with rotated labels "-01 Scheduling" to "-05 Payments". The annotated anatomy sheet is set on an aqua grain field. A gloop chain joins all five product icons into one connected pill (motion).

### 2.7 Pattern
- **Library:** named patterns, each with a two-to-three-word meaning and three adjectives, presented as navy-on-white strips on a cornflower field:
  - Balance: "Steady rhythm, clear structure". CONSISTENT, CALM, CLEAR (fine vertical bars)
  - Direction: "Dynamic movement". ANGULAR, ORDERED, BALANCED (alternating triangles)
  - Arc: "Motion without urgency". SOFT, RHYTHMIC, HUMAN (quarter-circle waves)
  - Presence: "Human moments". HUMAN, FOCUSED, INTENTIONAL (pill row)
  - Intervals: "Consistent shifts". SPACED, STRUCTURED, BREATHABLE (vertical and horizontal stripe blocks)
  - Open: "Space with intention". FLEXIBLE, SPACIOUS, AVAILABLE (offset horizontal stripes)
  - Phases: "Scaling over time". PROGRESSIVE, STEADY, COORDINATED (offset piano-key bars)
  - Flow: "Flexible at scale". ADAPTIVE, FLUID, COORDINATED (bars of varying weight)
- **Colourways:** each pattern is recoloured in the accent triplets (lilac/plum Presence, cornflower/Lake Flow, lime/avocado Direction, lime Open). Patterns tumble as rounded tiles in a motion piece.
- **Patternly** (Figma Make tool): presets Soft Lines, Quick Dash, Stacks, Offset Grid, Layered Grid, Arc. Controls: shape type, shapes per row, shape height, spacing, number of rows, row spacing, alternating pattern, horizontal offset, row height variance, colour (palette names such as "Cornflower 10"), Download SVG. The live preview shows the pattern **as a mask over a blurred gradient image**: white bars cut through a soft field so the image shows through the slots. This is the "pattern strip over blurred imagery" look.
- **Role:** "a simple graphic element between the background and the subject." It is a mid-ground layer that adds depth and recognition. It moves slowly behind product mockups "to pull the eye exactly to what matters most".
- **Origin:** Caleb Sun's idea of calendar blocks in a row. It was deliberately abstracted once the brand had to cover more than scheduling.

### 2.8 Motion
- **Rive** product animations (Caleb Sun learned Rive for this): tiny explainer loops, for example the Callie flow from a name chip to an email to suggested times to "Meeting Booked". Workflow cards rotate in 3D on navy.
- **Seen in GIFs:** concentric gradient rings ripple outward (the logo concept); the wordmark fades between states; product names scroll past on a wheel next to the wordmark (Scheduling, Payments, Contacts, Notetaker, Callie); bubbles gloop together into a loading indicator (dots swell and merge); a calendar list builds in; palette bands slide into proportion; pattern blocks swap between flat colour and blurred image; and the bento itself reshuffles tiles.
- **Motion character** *(inferred from frames)*: slow, eased, no bounce; things merge rather than snap.
- **Sonic:** none shown or mentioned.

### 2.9 UI fragments as brand elements
This is the system's strongest move. Real product data becomes the graphic language:
- **Flex-bubble chains** carry an icon, an event type, a status and times. On billboards they read as a headline ("Fitness Session Scheduled") and are also literal UI.
- **Time chips** like "9 – 9:30 am" and "11:30 – 12 pm" with colour bars, and the "15 minute buffer" line.
- **Booking/profile card:** a photo in a rounded frame, a huge two-line name in Neoris and Wulkan ("Kathy / Ardmore"), the role, and a [+][60 min] bubble.
- **Product-switcher pills:** a row of four icon squircles with the active one raised and gloop-joined to the card below. Used in web modules and ads.
- **Event cards:** blurred scenes with serif times.

## 3. Grids and layout

All measurements below are *(inferred)* from the published images at their delivered size.

### 3.1 Radius logic
One family of rounded rectangles runs from the logo outward. The squircle of the symbol, the app icon, the product icon containers, the flex bubbles, the gradient colour cards, the web cards, the Patternly window and the bento tiles are all soft-cornered rectangles with generous radii (roughly 6 to 10 percent of the shorter side on cards; full-round on bubbles). Nothing in the system has a sharp corner except the deliberately angular "Direction" pattern and the photo crops inside some bento tiles. **The logo's geometry drives the container language more than any column grid.**

### 3.2 Construction grids
The symbol and wordmark are shown on visible grids: a 5 by 5ish keyline grid around the squircle, and baseline, x-height and cap lines plus stem verticals through the wordmark. Showing the grid *is* part of the aesthetic. The type specimen uses horizontal guide bands behind each family name.

### 3.3 Ad and poster layouts
- **Centred stack:** symbol top-centre, two-line sans-then-serif headline centred, wordmark bottom-centre. Used for the tennis ad, the digital billboard and the half-hoarding. Very little else is on the page.
- **Split hoarding:** left half white with a centred stack in Cornflower; right half a photo with a pattern layer (Arc) behind the subject and a flex-bubble chain across it. This is one layout in two moods.
- **Digital screen:** a gradient ground; a white rounded bubble holds the headline; pattern bars bleed in from both sides at mid-height; the symbol is above and the wordmark below; tiny corner copy sits bottom-left (positioning line) and bottom-right (the five icons plus "Try Scheduling, Notetaker, Contacts, Callie and Payments today").
- **Sub-brand posters (portrait):** the photo or a paper texture fills the frame; a small symbol sits top-centre; the flex-bubble [icon][Product name] is at about 45 percent height, often overlapping the subject; the wordmark sits bottom-centre. Four posters, one per product, use the same skeleton.

### 3.4 Bento sheets
Two bentos are shown, and they work differently.
- **Hero bento (landscape 16:9, 1400 x 787):** three columns at about **1 : 2.1 : 1** (340 / 720 / 340 px). The centre column has three rows at about **1 : 3.4 : 1**. **No gutters:** tiles butt edge to edge, and the gradient, pattern and photo fields meet at hard seams. The **quiet tile is the biggest**: the centre is the app icon alone on Magnolia. The **dense tiles are the edges**: a purple pattern strip over blur, dashed ripple rings on navy, a bokeh field, the icon row on New Leaf, a portrait with pill shapes, the wordmark on Cornflower, a blue stripe pattern. The quiet centre and busy frame make the mark feel calm and the system feel rich at the same time.
- **Closing "Brand bento" (portrait 3:4, 1080 x 1440):** **Midnight #081D35 gutter and margin, both about 20 px** (about 1.9 percent of width), with rounded tiles. Rows are irregular: a 2-up top row (icons on gradient | cropped "6:25 Run" serif bubble), a middle block (iPhone icon crop and a photo bubble stacked left | the whole hero bento as one large tile right), and a bottom row (wordmark construction wide | Payments poster narrow). The hero bento is **nested** as one tile, so the deck quotes itself. Rhythm runs flat colour, photo, type, UI, construction, so no two neighbours share a medium.

### 3.5 One layout, many formats
The same skeleton (symbol top, headline in a bubble or on image centre, wordmark bottom, pattern as mid-ground) flexes from a 16:9 screen to a 3:1 street hoarding (split into two halves) to a 4:5 poster to a web module (bubble becomes card, pattern becomes the mockup backdrop). The **flex bubble is the flex mechanism**: it grows to hold any copy length, so layouts do not have to be redrawn per message.

### 3.6 Web modules
These are large white rounded cards on a gradient page ground. A product-switcher pill row is gloop-joined to the top of the card. The left half holds the label, headline (Neoris), body (Geist) and link; the right half holds a mockup on a tinted panel with a pattern behind it.

G: Callie product flow, then application pieces: tennis ad, indoor screen, type poster, profile card, type specimen, laptop product, hoarding, flex-bubble anatomy, 11:30/11:45, web module, G: loading and calendar list, Notetaker module, monitor crop, G: 3D workflow cards, G: testimonial carousel.
13. G: **Brand bento** closing frame.

**What makes it feel finished and modern:**
- **Whole system first, then parts, then the whole again.** Bento in, bento out, and the closing bento contains the opening one.
- **Half the figures move.** Motion is proof the system is alive, not a separate "motion" chapter.
- **Concept, construction, iteration, application** for each part: the reader sees the idea, the rigour (grids, Béziers), the search (iterations) and the payoff (in use).
- **Scale shifts:** a full-bleed poster, then a tight crop of an icon on a phone, then a 12-up board. The eye never settles into a rhythm of same-size images.
- **Mockups are restrained and real-feeling:** a laptop on a wooden sideboard with leaf shadow, an iPhone corner, a monitor corner, an indoor screen in a timber wall, a street hoarding. Flat sheets are used for system pages (palette, patterns, type). Mockups are only for applications.
- **Backgrounds are the brand:** almost every system slide sits on a brand gradient, a grain field or Midnight, never neutral grey.
- **Text is low and in a human voice.** It is a 16-minute read, but the images carry the story; the captions are two or three words ("Before | After", "Logo explo", "Brand bento"). The writing is candid about AI, killed work and the client process, which reads as confidence.
- **Showing a tool (Patternly)** signals that the system scales without the agency.

## 6. What reads as modern in 2026 (observable)

1. **Squircle everything**, including a glossy, lit, slightly 3D app icon in the current iOS idiom (inner glow, rim tint).
2. **Sans set-up plus serif payoff** in one headline, and serif numerals at display size.
3. **Real UI chips as headline graphics** (status bubbles, time ranges) instead of illustrations.
4. **Blurred, grainy colour fields** in place of flat brand colour or vector shapes. There is film grain on almost every gradient.
5. **Pattern as a mask over image**, so stripes reveal a blurred field, not stripes on flat colour.
6. **A named, purposeful pattern library** with mood words: a tool-first system, not a static guide.
7. **A custom generator (Patternly)** built in an AI prototyping tool, and shown publicly.
8. **AI imagery contained:** used for colour reference and backgrounds only, with the agency saying so.
9. **Bento as the summary format**, with nesting.
10. **Gloop:** soft liquid joins between shapes, a 2025-26 move away from hard geometric modularity.
11. **Restraint after a loud era:** the neon and confetti of 2021 replaced by calm pastels and lots of white (Magnolia) space.
12. **Leisure photography for a B2B tool:** the product's value shown as the life it frees up.

## 7. Transferable principles

1. **Name the gift, not the feature.** The concept is what the brand gives the user ("space in your day"), not what the product does ("scheduling"). *Evidence:* the client asked not to draw calendars; the mark became a ripple, not a date grid.
2. **One formal primitive, used at every scale.** Pick one shape behaviour and let it appear in the logo, the icons, the containers, the motion and the loading state. *Evidence:* the ripple squircle and gloop show up in the symbol, product icons, flex bubbles, loader, testimonial frames and bento tiles.
3. **Let the logo's geometry set the container language.** Derive corner radius and container shape from the mark before thinking about columns. *Evidence:* every card, bubble, tile and icon frame is a softened square like the symbol.
4. **Make real product data the graphic.** The most ownable graphic is a fragment of what the product actually outputs, set large. *Evidence:* [hourglass][Fitness Session][Scheduled] is both billboard headline and UI; "11:30 / 11:45" as hero type.
5. **Give each typeface one job and one emotion.** Sans for structure and set-up, serif for payoff, state and numbers, mono or grotesk for small product text. Pair them inside one line. *Evidence:* Neoris then Wulkan in every headline; Wulkan for "Scheduled" and times; Geist under 18 px.
6. **Treat colour as an environment, not swatches.** Define a small core (one hero, one ink, one off-white), add accent triplets (tint, mid, dark) per sub-brand, and name a gradient *category* drawn from real light. *Evidence:* Cornflower, Midnight, Magnolia; four triplets; "Environmental gradients".
7. **Design for print even if you live on screens.** Choose colours that reproduce on paper and large surfaces. *Evidence:* the 2021 neon pink "isn't printable", so it was dropped.
8. **Build a pattern library with meanings, then a tool to make it.** Name each pattern with a mood and three adjectives so non-designers pick by feeling; ship a generator so the system outlives the agency. *Evidence:* Balance, Direction, Arc, Presence, Intervals, Open, Phases, Flow; Patternly.
9. **Put pattern in the mid-ground.** Background, then pattern, then subject: the pattern adds depth and recognition without competing with the subject, and it can move slowly to steer the eye. *Evidence:* the Arc pattern behind the runner; the pattern drifting behind product mockups.
10. **Blur the image layer so type and UI can sit anywhere.** A softened image ground lets white bubbles and type sit on it without scrims and lets one image serve many formats. *Evidence:* blurred event cards; Patternly slicing blurred fields; type in white bubbles over gradients.
11. **Contain any generated or uncertain imagery to a supporting role.** If a medium is inconsistent, demote it to colour reference and backgrounds rather than banning it or letting it lead. *Evidence:* Midjourney output limited to backgrounds and colour references.
12. **One skeleton per format family, varied only by content.** Symbol top, message centre, wordmark bottom; swap the photo, product and pattern. *Evidence:* the four product posters, tennis ad, indoor screen and hoarding share one structure.
13. **Make the container elastic, not the layout.** A shape that stretches to hold any copy saves redrawing layouts. *Evidence:* flex bubbles hold any event name; "flexy gloop" also acts as connector, breadcrumb, photo holder and loader.
14. **Present whole, parts, whole; prove it moves.** Open and close on a bento, show concept, construction, iteration and application per part, and animate half the figures. *Evidence:* 48 figures, 24 animated, hero bento nested in the closing bento.
15. **Quiet centre, rich edges.** In summary sheets give the mark the biggest, emptiest tile and pack the system into the frame around it, mixing media so no neighbours repeat. *Evidence:* the hero bento's centre is the app icon alone on Magnolia, framed by pattern, photo, gradient and type tiles.

**A note on Ferry's own rules:** Calendly's boards use "fig 01." labels and "1 OF 12" counters. Ferry's presentation rule forbids corner labels and counters, so take the *staging* (iteration grids, construction sheets) without those labels. Calendly used AI image generation; Ferry's imagery rule forbids it. The transferable move is principle 10 plus 11: the hand-painted gouache plays the role Midjourney played. Blurred and cropped gouache becomes the environmental field that Patternly-style masks cut through, and the paintings themselves stay the hero.

## 8. Touchpoint checklist for a new brand to feel complete (by importance)

1. **Symbol, wordmark and lockups**, including construction sheet, iteration board and before/after if it is a rebrand.
2. **App icon / favicon in real OS context** (home screen, dock, browser tab).
3. **Core product UI screen(s)** with the new colour, type and container language applied, plus one empty state.
4. **Website hero and two or three modules** (product switcher, feature card, testimonial).
5. **A hero ad in 2 formats** (social 4:5 and 16:9) built on the shared skeleton.
6. **OOH:** one large (billboard or hoarding) and one indoor screen, to prove scale and print.
7. **Colour system slide** with names, hex values and proportion, shown in its environment.
8. **Type system:** specimen, roles, a headline pairing poster, and numerals or data in display type.
9. **Signature graphic device** (bubble, pattern, ripple) with an anatomy sheet and at least three applications.
10. **Pattern or texture library** with named moods, colourways, and the tool or recipe that makes it.
11. **Imagery direction:** a photo or art grid plus a "how it is treated" frame (blur, crop, grain, mask).
12. **Iconography or sub-brand marks**, with an anatomy sheet if there is more than one product.
13. **Motion:** logo reveal, one product explainer loop, one micro-interaction (loader or confirmation).
14. **Summary bento**, opening and closing.
15. **Customer or social proof module** (quote, stat, portrait).
16. **Email** (transactional receipt or confirmation, plus one marketing email). This is especially important for a payments brand, where the receipt is the most-seen surface. Calendly did not show this.
17. **Sales or pitch deck**: title, section and data slides.
18. **Social profile kit**: avatar, header, story and post templates.
19. **Print stationery and print collateral** (card, postcard, poster). Calendly did not show these.
20. **Events and merch** (lanyard, tote, signage). Calendly did not show these.
21. **Sonic logo / UI sounds** if the product has notifications or confirmations. Calendly did not show these.
