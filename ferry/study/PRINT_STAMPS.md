## Anatomy: every element of a stamp

Real stamps carry each element for a reason. The strong designer pieces keep almost all of them, which is why they read as stamps rather than as pictures with a wavy edge.

### 1. Paper, margin and centring

- **Margin:** a band of unprinted paper between the perforations and the design, roughly 6 to 10 percent of the stamp's width. It's white or cream: cream reads older and warmer (Mizzurna, Sakhalin), bright white reads modern and official (USPS).
- **Centring:** on real stamps the design sits slightly off centre, because the perforating machine never lines up perfectly. The Japanese Fuji stamp has a wider margin on the left than the right. Perfect centring reads digital; a slight drift reads printed.
- **Paper colour shows through** the lightest parts of the design. Nothing is pure white inside the design either.
- **Ageing on real ones:** toned cream paper, small brown foxing spots, ink faded unevenly, a hinge mark or gum stain on the back.

### 2. The edge: perforations

- **Perforated stamps** (classic): a row of round holes punched between stamps, then torn apart. What's left is a row of **teeth** with rounded **holes** between them. The torn tips are slightly ragged with paper fibres, not clean semicircles.
- **Hole size:** the hole is about half the pitch (the distance from one hole to the next), so teeth and holes are roughly equal width. Teeth that are too thin or holes too small look wrong immediately.
- **Gauge:** real stamps are described by holes per 2 cm, usually between 10 and 15 (14 is very common). At a typical stamp size that's roughly 12 to 20 teeth along a short side.
- **Corners:** a hole sits exactly at each corner, so every corner is a little notched tooth. Designs with consistent spacing land the corners cleanly. Uneven spacing that leaves a half-tooth at a corner is a giveaway of a sloppy or AI-made edge.
- **Die-cut wavy edge** (modern self-adhesives, like the USPS lighthouse): a smooth serpentine wave instead of holes. Cleaner, more contemporary, less nostalgic.
- **Imperforate or rouletted:** straight cut edges or small slits. Rare, very old or souvenir.
- **How the edge is shown:** the strong pieces set the stamp on a strongly contrasting ground (black, yellow, saturated blue or orange), so the perforation reads as a crisp negative shape. On a pale ground they add a soft shadow under the stamp.

### 3. The frame and window

- **Keyline:** one or two thin printed lines just inside the margin, framing the design (Aritzia has a double keyline; Mumbai a hairline). It's what makes the printed area feel "issued".
- **Window shape:** rectangle (most), rounded rectangle (Mizzurna), oval (Bermuda, Haida Gwaii, Prince Rupert), arch (Greece), or full bleed to the margin (Kitsune, Tokyo Blues).
- **Ornamental frames** on classic stamps: scrolls, beaded borders and corner ornaments (Bermuda), pattern panels (Japan). Modern designers replace ornament with type bands.
- **Bands and tablets:** solid bars that hold the name or value (Kitsune's blue band, Mumbai's blue panels, US Postage's lower panel). They give the type a stable home and the design a grid.

### 4. The image

Six ways the picture is made:

| Technique | Look | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Line engraving (intaglio) | Fine parallel and crossing lines build tone; one or two colours; crisp and raised | Bermuda caravel, Sakhalin wave-lines |
| Typography or letterpress | Flat areas, slight ink squash at edges, small breaks | Japan Fuji |
| Flat colour, screen-print or litho | 3 to 6 flat colours, no gradients, shapes overlap | US Postage mountain, Canadian set |
| Halftone and grain | Dots or speckle, ink broken by paper texture | Aritzia shell, Mumbai |
| Painting | A miniature painting, soft and full colour | USPS Hillsboro Inlet |
| Photo collage | Cut-out photo objects on a flat colour field | Traffic light with birds |

- **One subject, centred or slightly off,** with lots of air. Even at 30 mm the stamp has a clear focal point.
- **Limited colour:** most of the best are one to three inks (blue and orange Kitsune, blue Sakhalin, brown Bermuda). Monochrome reads valuable and official.
- **Scale tricks:** tiny figures beside a huge object (Tokyo Blues tuba with ladders and people) give a miniature world.

### 5. Text elements (the anatomy most designers forget)

| Element | Where it sits | How it's set | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issuer or country | Top band or bottom band; on classic stamps, always | Serif caps with spacing, or bold grotesque | BERMUDA, USA, 大日本帝國郵便, РОССИЯ |
| Denomination (value) | A corner, often in a tablet or alone and large | Big numerals; the unit small beside or under (c, d, SN, руб.) | ¼d, 20 SN, 37 USA, 46c, 25, 10 |
| Subject caption | Bottom, centred, small | Small serif caps, wide spacing | HILLSBORO INLET, FLORIDA |
| Year of issue | A short vertical line along one edge, or very small at the bottom | Tiny, rotated 90 degrees | 2003 (USPS), 2025 (US Postage) |
| Designer or printer imprint | Bottom edge, smallest text on the stamp | Tiny caps | DESIGN BY GK.DSN |
| Series marker | A corner or the caption line | Numbers or "Collection 01" | 01, 02, 3/30, COLLECTION 01 |
| Second language | Under or beside the main name | Smaller, a different script | Kitsune / 狐, Mizzurna Falls / ミザーナフォールズ |
| Secondary data | Under the caption | Small spaced caps | Coordinates: 46.01875° N · 143.41398° E |
| Colour dots | A corner, beside the date | Small solid circles in the stamp's inks | Mizzurna's blue and grey dots, like printer registration swatches |

- **Hierarchy is strict:** one big element (the value or the name), one medium (the subject), and two or three tiny ones (year, series, imprint). The tiny lines are what make a design read as an official issue.
- **Numerals are display type.** The denomination is often the largest type on the stamp and set in its own style (Didone numerals on Canadian stamps, bold grotesque on Kitsune).
- **Type follows the frame:** arcs on ovals (Prince Rupert, Haida Gwaii), straight bands on rectangles, vertical text up a side panel (Japan, Tokyo Blues).

### 6. Postmarks and cancellations

- **Circular date stamp:** an outer ring with the town or office along the arc, the date in the middle on one or two lines, and often a double ring (London W.1, 12 SEP 1948). Some carry a time.
- **Cancel lines:** four to six parallel wavy lines beside the circle, the "killer" that cancels the stamp (Salem MA, Sakhalin 2026).
- **Pictorial cancels and cachets:** a drawing inside the ring (Black Ship 1853's ship, the Bremerhaven three-master).
- **Ink:** black, red, violet or blue. It's never even: the strike is lighter on one side, broken where the paper dipped, slightly doubled, and the ink spreads into the fibres.
- **Placement is the realism cue:** the postmark crosses the stamp's edge onto the paper or background (Sakhalin's postmark runs off the stamp onto the yellow; the Argentina cancel crosses the perforations). A postmark contained inside the stamp looks fake.
- **Rotation:** postmarks land at an angle, never perfectly level.
- **Other marks:** "First Day of Issue", "Par Avion / By Air Mail" labels, boxed rubber stamps ("BY AIR MAIL"), and red and blue airmail chevrons around covers.

### 7. Print texture, the small details that sell it

- Paper speckle and fibre visible through flat colour (Aritzia, Mumbai, the Mumbai orange background).
- Ink density varies across a flat area, darker at edges where ink pooled.
- Slight misregistration between colours, a hair of paper showing between two inks.
- Edges of shapes slightly broken, not vector-perfect.
- Grain on the background the stamp sits on, so the whole image shares one material.
- Never a smooth digital gradient. Where there's tone, it's made of lines, dots or brush marks.

## Layout systems for a series

- **Lock the template, change only the picture, colour and words.** The ancient-capitals stamps from the main board study, the Japanese-inspired series on black, Kitsune and Manaus all hold every element in the same place across the set.
- **Typical templates:**
  - Image window on top, type band at the bottom with name, subtitle, and a year with colour dots (Mizzurna).
  - Big name top left, value top right, image centre, coloured band at the bottom with two place names (Kitsune).
  - Title strip across the top, image, a strip of supporting detail, then a bottom row with a big glyph and three lines of small copy (Tokyo Blues).
- **Variation that stays coherent:** different window shapes inside the same palette and paper (Canadian set), or one colour per stamp on the same template (ivivu from the main board).
- **Numbering** turns a set into a collection: 01 to 06, Collection 01, 3/30.

## Presentation patterns

1. **One stamp on a strong flat ground** with small editorial type around it: a title, a counter (3/30), a credit line, and a large faint ghost word behind (Sakhalin). The stamp is small in the frame; the frame is a poster.
2. **On black** for maximum punch, the perforations crisp white against the dark (Mizzurna, Kitsune, the Japanese series).
3. **On a colour from the stamp** for harmony (Canadian set on sky blue, US Postage on blue).
4. **Detail crops** at huge scale showing the perforations, paper grain and ink (Mumbai, Aritzia corners). These prove craft.
5. **Held in hands or on fabric** for physical proof (Oregon and Vermont in the main board).
6. **Sets in a grid** with even gutters, or loosely overlapping and rotated (FIFA stack).
7. **Used:** on an envelope with a postmark, or postmarks crossing onto the ground.

## AI tells to avoid

- Garbled glyphs where the corner ornaments or small text should be (the Lisbon stamp's corner icons are nonsense marks).
- Perforations with uneven spacing, half teeth at corners, or teeth that change size along a side.
- Soft generic vector clip-art (lighthouse plus gulls plus waves) in identical tile grids.
- Postmark text that can't be read, or handwriting that's scribble.
- Everything perfectly centred and perfectly clean, with no paper, no drift, no ink spread.
- Missing anatomy: no value, no issuer, no year. Without them it's a picture with a wavy border, not a stamp.

## Checklist for a Ferry stamp

1. Paper margin in cream, design slightly off centre.
2. Real perforation geometry: holes about half the pitch, a hole at each corner, even count per side, ragged tips.
3. A keyline frame and a deliberate window shape.
4. One clear image, made in one honest technique (for Ferry: a Paint gouache painting, or an engraved-line version).
5. Issuer, value, subject caption, year, series number and imprint, each at its own level of the hierarchy.
6. Print texture: paper speckle, uneven ink, a hair of misregistration.
7. When used: a postmark that crosses the edge, at an angle, unevenly inked.
8. Presented as a close-up: the stamp on envelope paper that bleeds off the frame, with soft light falling from the top left. No added labels, counters or explainer lines: the stamp already carries its text.

## Open questions for implementation

1. **Faithful or interpreted?** Real-stamp anatomy (value, issuer, year, imprint), or a modern designer take that keeps the shape and drops some of it?
2. **What is the value?** A stamp needs a denomination. For Ferry it could be the fee ("0.08"), the crossing time ("41s"), a block number, or a plain series value.
3. **What is the issuer?** "FERRY" in the issuer slot, or the port name (the chain) there and Ferry as the imprint.
4. **The edge:** classic perforations, the modern die-cut wave, or Ferry's own stepped notch from the mark.
5. **The image technique:** full-colour Paint gouache (warm, painterly), or a one-ink engraved-line version (official, valuable), or both: gouache for heroes, engraved for small sizes and postmarks.

## Decisions (Nov 11, 2026)

- **Anatomy:** faithful. Every Ferry stamp carries issuer, value, subject caption, year, series number and imprint.
- **Value:** the crossing time, set as the big numeral (for example "41s"), so the denomination states the promise: arrives on time.
- **Edge:** Ferry's own stepped notch, taken from the mark's geometry, instead of round perforations. It still has to follow the perforation rules: even spacing, a notch at each corner, a consistent count per side, slightly ragged tips.
- **Image technique:** both. Paint gouache for hero stamps; a one-ink engraved-line version for small sizes, sheets and postmarks.
- **Presentation (user feedback):** no editorial type around a stamp. The Sakhalin-style frame of titles, counters and credits is rejected for Ferry, because the stamp and its postmark already carry every word.
- **Presentation (user choice):** the close-up. Scale the object until its paper bleeds off the frame, and add a soft directional light. Chosen over a faded painting behind it, and over a small stamp on a flat ground, which read as empty.
