# Sajon and Lucie Bajgart: study for Ferry

Method note. Read on X in the user's Chrome, read-only. Both designers post mostly videos, so I studied frames of the videos (zoomed at several timestamps) and the stills of the one image carousel. Anything not clearly visible is marked (inferred). Not studied: the posts' source files, any website. The client's "Chiron" is almost certainly the brand **Kyron** (Sajon, posted Nov 10 and Nov 11, 2026): painted Greek temples, a wax seal, bronze coins, stamps, a sign-off of "currency". No post named Chiron exists in X search for from:sajon_co, and WebSearch found no case study, Behance or Dribbble page for Kyron. The site kyron.com was reported not working in a reply, so there is no site to read.

Post budget actually studied: Lucie 4 posts or sets, Sajon 8 posts or sets (Kyron 2, others 6).

## Chiron in depth (Kyron)

Brand: Kyron, a digital currency or blockchain brand. Tag line variants seen: "A Different Kind of Currency.", "The Next Chapter of Currency.", "A New Standard Of Digital Value.", "Welcome to a New Era of Currency.", "Currency in Focus." Positioning: "bridge classic monetary heritage with digital value to build instant institutional trust" (Sajon's own wording, S2). This is the same position as Ferry: classic trusted base, one modern layer.

**The mark.** A bold plus sign built as an isometric, impossible-looking cross: each arm is an outlined bar whose ends are cut as if folded, so it reads as a Penrose style 3D cross. It is used as: a debossed relief on the coin and the seal, a white outline on olive, a small white sticker, a tiny mark beside the wordmark in the middle of the painted landscape, the symbol on the jacket and banners. The wordmark "Kyron" is lowercase-capital K then lowercase, set in a medium geometric sans, next to the symbol, small.

**Type.** Two or three families, strictly roles.
- Display: a light, high-contrast transitional serif with very tight tracking and tight leading, sentence case. It is set huge, cropped by the tile edge ("The History of Money is Still Being Written." runs off both left and right edges on a pale blue tile), in cream on pale blue, or in pale blue on cream ("A Different Kind of Currency." in light blue on cream, half hidden behind the coin). Type is so large it behaves as texture. Weight is regular or light, not bold.
- Subhead and cards: the same serif at about 20 percent of the display size, e.g. "The Bronze Obol", "The Attic Drachma", "The Roman Denarius", "The Kyron Coin", with a tiny line of mono type above each ("680 BC LYDIA", "450 BC ATHENS", "211 BC ROME", "2026 AD ON-CHAIN").
- Mono: a small monospace for captions, url and fine print ("www.kyron.com", "The Next Chapter Of Currency.", body on banners). Used at very small sizes, upper and lowercase mixed, tracked normally.
- Numerals 1 2 3 4: huge, light serif numerals at the bottom of the timeline cards, in grey, overlapping the painting on card 3 (cream numeral over the temple painting).
- Part of the banner type stays pale blue or grey for emphasis, with only one word coloured ("Currency." in paler grey on banners and posters, the rest dark). A "tonal tail" move: the last word of a headline is lighter.

**Palette.** Taken from the world, not arbitrary: pale sky blue (the sky on the painting), warm cream (marble and paper), olive-sage grey-green (the ground in two tiles), deep umber brown (the stamp and sticker tile, the coin's shadow side), bronze for coins. No pure black, no gradients. All of these are in the Greek painting (sky, stone, olive trees, shadowed bronze).

**The painted piece.** The only painted tile is the wide landscape in the top centre: an old-master watercolour with a pale sky, clouds, two ruined or standing Ionic colonnades framing the view, a small round temple in the middle distance on a hill, a lake and blue mountains, a few cypress trees, warm ochre stone. The Kyron logo sits dead centre in the sky, small, dark. It is clearly painted, soft edged, with layers of washed blue to ochre. It is the "origin" image: the other tiles borrow its pieces. (I could not tell whether it is a painting or a generated image; the client's rules for Ferry forbid generated images, so treat as reference only.)

**Photographed or rendered real things, and how painting meets them.**
- A tall poster: a photograph of two real Ionic columns under a blue sky with ruined walls, a cream banner hanging between them with "A Different Kind of Currency." and the torn coin. The banner is a mockup in a real photo. The painting appears nowhere in this photo.
- A wide banner on the Colosseum arches, cream, with "Welcome to a New Era of Currency." and a small **painted temple card** pasted on it (a rectangular cut of the painting, with a pale blue slab behind it as an offset block). This is the exact "painted world mixed with real" moment: a real photograph of a Roman ruin, a cream banner printed with a layout in the brand, and inside that layout the painted temple as an image. The painted image gets a pale blue offset rectangle behind it, which gives it paper-collage depth. It is flat on the banner, so the banner's fold or rippling in the video carries realism.
- A studio still life: a cream book titled "The Next Chapter of Currency." leaning on a warm sandstone ruin: fluted column fragment, carved acanthus panel, a weathered Corinthian capital, with a late golden side light and deep shadows. The coin is torn out of the book cover, as a sticker (see coin).
- A model in a cream hoodie seen from behind with "Currency in Focus." printed on the back, where the "o" is replaced by the small bronze coin. A real cap on the model. The coin glyph replaces a letter, so the product and the brand object join inside a word.
- A poster pair on olive: left cream poster with three bronze coins floating at different sizes, right a dark umber poster with the painted round temple crop and one coin balanced on its roof. Both titled "A New Standard Of Digital Value.". The framed poster sits centred in an olive tile with wide margins and visible slivers of the next poster at the tile edge (carousel).
- A frame of the painted arch: an olive tile with a crop of the painting, an arched stone window onto the sea, and the outlined plus symbol floating in the middle of the opening.

**Stamps.** A strip of postage stamps on umber: each stamp is cream with an engraved-style (monochrome sepia line drawing) building: a pediment temple, a round tholos, fluted columns, with a deep **scalloped perforated edge** (cream with a ring of circular cut-outs, showing the umber behind). Each has a denomination numeral in a small cream label bottom left ("10", "20", "30") and a tiny label right ("Heliox/Mexico" or similar, unreadable) plus a tiny "Postage" word top left and a value top right. The stamp is wide, nearly square, the drawing takes about 70 percent, the label strip about 20 percent. They scroll horizontally like a film strip in the video, cropped at the tile edge on both sides so the strip implies more.

**Stickers.** On the umber tile, four die-cut stickers with a white border and a soft drop shadow at random angles: a capital, a coin, the plus mark, a temple facade. White border, line-drawn interior. They scatter into place in the animation (inferred).

**Seals.** A dark brown wax seal about 45 percent of the tile width on the pale blue type tile. It has a thick irregular rim with a raised ridge, glossy highlights, a recessed disc with the plus symbol embossed (raised relief, with a fine outline on the symbol). It sits at a slight angle overlapping the headline letters, so the word "Money" is partly under the wax. Cast shadow is soft and close. This is the strongest single image: paper, type and a physical object.

**Layout and presentation.** The primary format is a 3x3 tile grid of unequal tiles on a white field, with rounded-corner tiles (about 8 to 10 px radius at this size) and an even gutter (about 10 px). Large tile top centre (painting), square tiles at the sides, a double-width tile on the bottom row. The tile contents change during the 11 second loop: the olive square at top right flips between an outlined plus on olive, plus on umber, and the hoodie photo; the bottom row changes between the still life, the arch banner, the Colosseum banner, and the four-card timeline. The centre tile (cream with the torn coin over the headline) stays constant as the anchor. So the grid is a live mood board: a fixed anchor, rotating satellites. The carousel (S2) shows only 3 tall tiles: seal over big type, two posters on olive, torn coin over cream.

**The world built across the set.** Every tile reads as one place: a Greek or Roman hill town and its postal and mint offices. The same five motifs recur: columns and temples (paint, photo, stamp, sticker), the bronze coin, the plus symbol relief, a cream paper ground, a blue sky. The type moves are repeated tile to tile. The timeline card "680 BC Lydia, 450 BC Athens, 211 BC Rome, 2026 AD On-chain" is the narrative spine: it puts Kyron in the history of money, which gives the "classic base, modern layer" argument explicitly.

**Reception (replies).** "Coins look like oreo cookies, this is not giving 'monetary heritage' vibes" (a design account, Nov 11), "this is smooth", and a note that kyron.com did not work. So the coin's central relief reads as a biscuit pattern at small size when it is dark bronze and the symbol is deep: lesson in the coin section.

## The coin

URL: https://x.com/sajon_co/status/2120003538097221810 (Nov 10, photo 3 shows the coin large; photo 2 shows three floating; photo 1 shows the wax seal). Also in https://x.com/sajon_co/status/2120547427858125123 at the centre tile and on the poster.

Both faces: only one face is shown, the obverse. The reverse is not shown. (inferred) The edge is not shown either, since the coin is photographed from slightly off-axis, with a thin visible rim as a thickness of about 10 to 15 percent of the diameter in the three-coin poster.

What is on it. A circular bronze disc, matte-to-satin warm copper-brown, with a raised outer rim. In the centre the plus symbol is **deeply debossed with a raised outline**: the mark's isometric edges are cut as a chamfered groove, so the arms look like carved channels, with a lighter ridge on the bevelled edges. The surface has a very fine pitting and mottled patina (darker brown in recesses, more orange on high points), like aged bronze, not polished gold. Visible wear spots near the rim.

Text on the rim. None legible on the coin itself. The text is on the **torn paper wrapper** in the centre tile: a white or cream paper peels back at the top and bottom of the coin, like a label tearing off a rolled coin or a sticker, with the words "KYRON", "DIGITAL CURRENCY", "BLOCKCHAIN", "NFT", "KRN" repeating on a diagonal in small black caps, as if a printed security band. So the "text on the rim" is the repeating security-band wording on the torn paper, not on the metal. (inferred: the paper is a flat 2D overlay on a rendered disc.)

Metal and finish. Bronze, warm, satin, with a soft specular on the top left rim and a darker lower right edge. Highlights are broad, not hard. No foil, no gold shine.

How it is made or shot. Almost certainly a 3D render (a clean disc with the symbol as a bump or displacement map, soft studio light, plain background) that is then composited: (a) floating at three sizes on a flat cream poster with soft contact-free shadows (the coins float, there is no table); (b) balanced on the roof of the painted temple on the poster, so a 3D object sits on a painted building, which is the key combine; (c) torn out of paper in a mockup with a still-life light; (d) tiny, as a glyph replacing the "o" in the printed phrase "Currency in Focus." on a hoodie. Lighting is key light from the upper left. Scale is variable: giant over a headline, small as a letter, stuck to a painting. Shadows are soft and close or absent.

What it is used for in the brand: the hero object and the "proof of value". It is the one tangible thing, it is the iconic image of the poster pair, it is the seal of the system (the wax seal is the same plus on a different material), and it is the glyph inside copy. The seal and the coin are the same symbol in wax and bronze, which makes the world feel crafted.

Weakness. At small sizes the central relief is lost and the bumpy surface reads as a biscuit (the "oreo" comment). The mark needs more flat, outline value and a lighter inner field to read.

## What both do that makes it work

1. **Type is the main character, scaled to bleed.** Lucie's headlines are the biggest thing on a panel (L1). Sajon sets "The History of Money is Still Being Written." so large it is cropped at both edges (S1, S2 photo 1), then a seal sits on it. Big, light serif, tight tracking, little weight.
2. **One painted source, many borrowed crops.** Kyron has one painting (colonnade and temple); its pieces reappear as stamp drawings, sticker icons, a pasted card on the banner, a poster tile, and the arch tile (S1). Lucie has one painted house and butterfly set reused across panels (L1, L3, L4).
3. **A physical object sits on top of the painting, not inside it.** The coin on the painted temple roof (S2 photo 2) and the paper card on the butterfly (L2) are crisp and flat objects over soft paint. Scale mismatch is the point.
4. **Real photography is the stage, the brand is a printed object on it.** Banners on a Colosseum, a poster between two real columns, a hoodie on a model, a book on a ruin (S1). The real world is mundane; the brand objects are the novelty.
5. **Object families, not one mockup.** Seal, coin, stamps, stickers, poster, banner, hoodie, book: eight things in one 11 second video, all with one symbol and one palette (S1).
6. **A fixed anchor tile, rotating satellites.** The coin-on-headline tile stays while the rest rotate (S1). Gives a loop that feels designed.
7. **Palette from the world.** Kyron: sky blue, cream, olive, umber, bronze, all in the painting. Lucie: sky, sea blue, flower orange, grass green, white.
8. **A narrative spine inside the type.** Kyron's four-card timeline puts the brand in the history of money (S1, 0:08). It says why the old look is on a modern product.
9. **Small round mark as a stamp.** Lucie's tiny butterfly roundel (L1, L4); Sajon's coin as a letter glyph in "Currency in Focus." (S1).
10. **Restraint at the edges.** Wide margins, one accent, one object per tile, a one-line caption. Video grid on white with rounded tiles (S1, L4).

## What Ferry could take

**Objects that could exist as tangible pieces.**
- **A ferry token (a coin or medallion).** Yes, a coin fits a ferry service: ferry tokens, transit tokens, and tickets stamped on boarding are real historical things (harbour toll tokens). A ferry-service token is more natural than for a currency brand, because it stands for a fare or a crossing, not for money itself. Spec: a round brass or pewter token, one face with the stepped two-shore mark in relief, the other with a small lighthouse or a ferry hull and "FERRY" in Ferry Sans on the rim, a hole or notch optional. Edge: reeded or plain. Show both faces on a post, and the edge. Keep the relief shallow and the inner field flat so it reads small (learn from the "oreo" reply). Use it as: the transfer-complete object, the ticket's "stamp of passage", and the glyph in a headline.
- **A wax seal or embossed seal** with the stepped mark, pressed on a postcard flap or the transfer chip. Dark umber or the painting's deep blue-green. Over type like Kyron's seal (S2 photo 1).
- **The stamp strip**, already in Ferry, in a sliding strip cropped at both edges. Use the real perforation as an alpha cut with the dark ground showing through.
- **Die-cut stickers** of lighthouse, mooring post, ferry, the mark, with white borders and a soft shadow, scattered.
- **A paper wrapper band** (like Kyron's torn band) around a token, printed in repeating tiny caps with route names or chain names in Ferry Sans. This is a good home for the "chain" names.
- **A postcard** with a postmark partly over the stamp, a lined address side in the script, and a punched ticket edge (borrow Lucie's punched dots as ticket-stub holes).
- **The ticket chip** with a stepped notch, printed on warm paper stock, a tiny mono-type line above each title (Kyron timeline pattern) such as "ROUTE 1 CHAIN A TO CHAIN B".

**How painted and real can meet (no AI imagery).**
- Painted ferry and harbour as the ground, the token or stamp as a crisp rendered or photographed object resting on a painted dock or balanced on a painted lighthouse roof (S2 photo 2).
- A real photographed paper object (postcard, ticket) lying across the painting with an offset coloured block behind it (the pale blue slab on the Colosseum banner).
- The painting printed on a real banner or poster, photographed in a real harbour (S1).
- A paper notecard with punched holes over the gouache crop (L2), set at a few degrees, cropped by the frame.

**Type moves.**
- Ferry Sans display set huge and bled off both edges with a seal or coin on top, one word in a lighter tone at the end ("Cross" bold, "chains." light).
- A tiny mono or Ferry Sans caption above titles with place and year (Kyron's "680 BC LYDIA"). For Ferry: harbour name or chain and date, to anchor the story.
- Huge light numerals 1 to 4 over the painting for a "route in four stops" card.
- A glyph replacement: the mark or the coin in place of the "o" in a short line on a tote or cap.

**Presentation formats.**
- A 3x3 video grid with an anchor tile and satellites, rounded tiles, white or paper ground (S1, L4).
- A 3 to 4 tall-image carousel (S2) with seal over type, poster pair, token over headline.
- One stage per object: a single token on pure ground with a long margin (like S6), then the same on a painted ground.
- A timeline card strip: the history of crossing (ferries, tolls, tokens, on-chain).

**Brand world.** Pick a place and keep five recurring motifs (for Ferry: harbour, lighthouse, ferry, stepped shore mark, stamp and postmark) and make every object a government office of that place: stamp, seal, token, ticket, postcard.

## What not to copy

- **AI-generated source images.** Lucie says her butterflies were generated; the Kyron painting may be. Ferry's rule is no AI image generation; the gouache stays the true source.
- **The unreadable coin relief.** Kyron's deep, busy bronze relief reads as a biscuit at small size and drew a public "oreo" reply. Keep Ferry's token relief shallow, flat inner field, strong outline.
- **A symbol-only coin with no text.** Add the brand name and a rim line or the wording lives only on the paper band (as in Kyron), which makes the metal feel anonymous.
- **Generic mockup stages** (hoodie, cap, Colosseum banner) used because they exist. Pick objects belonging to a ferry service.
- **Too many objects in one frame.** Kyron's grid works as a video because it rotates; as a still it is busy. Show fewer at a time.
- **Dark umber and bronze palette.** It is Kyron's from its painting. Ferry's palette comes from its gouache; do not take Kyron's colours.
- **The Penrose cross symbol and Greek columns.** Kyron's own. Take the method, not the motifs.
- **Hype captions with fake-sounding stats** ("73% of investors dismiss...") if not sourced.
- **Isometric or impossible geometry in the mark** unless the stepped two-shore mark gets it on its own terms.
