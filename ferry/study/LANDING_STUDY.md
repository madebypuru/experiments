## What the category shares (so Ferry can differ)

1. Dark ground or white ground, with one electric accent (mint on Across, violet on Relay, lilac on Phantom, orange on incident.io). Seen on 5 of 6 bridge and wallet sites.
2. The headline promises speed: "Instantly", "in seconds", "1.2S", "fast by default". Relay, Across, deBridge ("fastest way"), Wormhole. Nobody promises safety first in the hero; Across and Wormhole put it in feature rows ("battle-tested", "secure").
3. The hero is either the bridge widget itself (Across, Wormhole's Portal lower down) or an abstract 3D object (Across's glowing cylinders, Phantom's coins and diamonds). No place, no people, no weather, no craft.
4. Proof is numbers in large type: $40B+, 5M, 1.2S (Across); $20B+, 85+ chains, 99.9 percent (Relay); $68,000,000,000 (Wormhole). The numbers are volume and speed. Few show a live transfer with a real state.
5. Partner logos as proof (Uniswap, MetaMask, Coinbase, Phantom). Quotes are rare (Across has one).
6. Two buttons: a coloured primary ("Start swapping") and a black "Go to docs" (Relay, Wormhole, deBridge). Wallet-connect as the only action on Across.
7. A code snippet in the page for developers (Across, Relay via docs links). Footer carries Docs, API, Testnets.
8. The tone is rising institutional: LayerZero ("underpins all of global finance"), Wormhole ("financial infrastructure for the modern economy"), Squads (now "stablecoin economy ... not a bank"). Everyone is moving from "bridge" to "infrastructure" and "payments".
9. Types: one neutral grotesque, sometimes with a serif or mono for flavour. No image of a place anywhere.

Ferry can differ by: leading with safety and a visible crossing instead of speed; a painted place instead of a 3D object; a paper ticket instead of a widget as the live proof; one dark pill nowhere; one sans only; seconds shown as a plain number, not a slogan; a calm, specific voice.

## What looks dated or cheap

- Glossy 3D coins, diamonds and glowing cylinders (Phantom, Across): they read as crypto marketing of 2021 to 2024 and make the hero about decoration.
- Large dark gradients with neon accents on near-black (the whole bridge category).
- "Instantly", "lightning-fast", "No friction. No waiting." as headlines: they say nothing checkable.
- Lazy-loaded blocks that leave blank screens between sections (Relay and LayerZero left a full empty viewport during scroll), and fully faded hero text at load (Family): the first view looks broken.
- A cookie banner with a joke (PostHog) and a news banner stacked over the nav (Stripe, Sketch): clutter above the fold.
- Frosted glass cards overlapping a subject (Monarch, CHIP_STUDY).
- Walls of social posts as proof (deBridge): noisy and unverifiable.
- Forecast charts with optimistic and conservative lines (Wormhole): reads as a pitch deck, not a product.
- Product "legends" that have wound down (Legend, Arc): do not cite as live references.

Notes for the build:
- The ticket is the one thing that keeps appearing: in the hero, in the how-it-works panel, in the board and in the failure example. That repetition gives a bridge page the coherence that the category's separate widget, stats and logos lack.
- Signal appears once per view: on the hero ticket's position dot; on the failure ticket in section 5; on the closing button only if the viewport does not also show a Signal element.
- Keep each headline to 3 to 6 words; the largest type on the page is the hero headline only (about 7 percent of viewport width); no bleed headlines on this page except possibly the closing.
- Only the numbers that exist on launch (audit names, uptime, crossings) may be shown; mark any placeholder figure as such in the build brief.
