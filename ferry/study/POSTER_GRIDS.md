# Poster grids and the details around them

A second pass over the "2 hour brand exercise" board (all 27 pins, unchanged since the first study), looking only at how each poster is built: margins, axes, columns, zones and proportions, and what the small details around the main content do. Each pin was measured over a 5 percent ruler grid, then the reading was drawn back over the poster to check it lines up. Numbers are fractions of the poster's width (x) or height (y). Pin numbers match `BOARD_STUDY.md`.

## The grids that repeat

Seven grid types cover every poster on the board.

1. **One axis, type in a corner** (Empee). A single left axis about 10% in. The type block sits in the bottom quarter, the image takes the rest and bleeds. Editorial and confident.
2. **Centre axis, split in two** (Café Magnético). Type in the top half, image in the bottom half, 10% side margins. Ceremonial, poster-like.
3. **Centre axis, stacked zones** (Jupi, You-nique, Flowwow). Headline, image, footnote, each in its own band, with the widest gap before the smallest text. Calm and readable.
4. **Image field over a footer band** (Morocco). Roughly 70/30. The footer holds identity and facts in two columns and a bottom row. The mark straddles the seam.
5. **One object, centred, over its own world** (Fieldman, Odd Sauce). The object takes about 68% of the width with equal 12% margins above and below. The background is a faded photo of where it comes from.
6. **Cards or tiles on a grid** (Popcorn, Calendly). Even gutters about 2 to 2.5%, one corner radius, tiles spanning different widths for rhythm.
7. **A template repeated** (capitals stamps, Messy Studio). The same positions for title, value, seal and caption on every item.

## Proportions that repeat

- **Margins:** 10% of the width for full-bleed posters (Empee, Café). About 12% top and bottom when one object sits on a ground (Fieldman, Odd Sauce).
- **The object on a ground** takes 58 to 68% of the width (Morocco 58, Fieldman and Odd Sauce 68).
- **Type to image split:** half and half (Café), 72/28 (Morocco), identity in the top quarter (You-nique), type in the bottom quarter (Empee).
- **Headline measure:** about 55 to 65% of the width, never full width.
- **Headline height** is about 2 to 3 times the subline, and the subline sits right under it on the same axis.
- **Gaps grow toward the small text.** The space before a footnote is wider than the space before the image.

## Details around the content

This is the most consistent finding. **Almost nothing sits around a poster's content on this board.** There are no corner labels, counters, credits or ghost words. Every small detail lives inside the piece, and each does a job:

| Detail | Job | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A small logo | Says who | On the axis: top centre (You-nique), bottom centre (Flowwow), or in the footer column (Morocco) |
| An arrow in a circle | Says "go" | Under the message, centred (Café) |
| A repeated word ribbon | Says the format ("Virtual") and adds rhythm | Across the top edge (Café) |
| A category and a URL | Say which series and where to go | Bottom corners of the footer, one baseline (Morocco) |
| A value, a seal, a caption | Make a stamp official | The stamp's four corners (capitals) |
| A white border round a photo | Says "this is a print" | About 2% inside the card (Fieldman) |
| A mark straddling a seam | Ties two zones together | Between image and footer (Morocco) |
| Neighbouring cards peeking in | Says "there's more" | Both sides of the frame (Jupi) |
| The world behind | Says where the object comes from | A faded full-bleed photo (Fieldman, Odd Sauce) |

The rule this gives Ferry: **a detail earns its place only if it tells the viewer something the piece doesn't already say.** The corner labels removed from stamp 01 failed that test; Morocco's "Culture" and URL pass it.

## For Ferry

- The grids above all work with Ferry's material: gouache paintings bleed well (types 1, 2, 3), the print pieces suit type 5 (one object over its world, which is close to the chosen stamp close-up), and the bento layouts in the brief are type 6.
- The mark can straddle a seam (type 4), and the stepped notch can do the job of a perforation line or a divider.
- The details Ferry would actually need are few: the logo (who), a crossing time or a route (the promise), and a URL or call to action when the poster asks for something.

## Decisions (Nov 11, 2026)

- **Formats first:** 4:5 social posts and 9:16 stories.
- **Grid types Ferry keeps:**
  1. **Object over its world** (Fieldman, Odd Sauce): a print piece centred at about 68% of the width, with equal margins above and below, over its world. This is the family the stamp close-up belongs to.
  2. **One axis, type in a corner** (Empee): a gouache painting bleeding off the frame, with a short headline parked bottom-left on a 10% axis.
  3. **Centre axis, stacked zones** (Jupi, You-nique): headline, painting and footnote on one centre line, with the widest gap before the smallest text.
- **Not adopted:** the image field with a footer band, the centre split, cards on a grid (kept for the bento layouts in the brief, not posters), and the repeated template (already used inside the stamps).
- **Details:** only those that tell the viewer something new. Usually the logo, the promise (a time or a route), and a URL or call to action when the poster asks for something.

## Templates (built Nov 11, 2026)

`print/posters.html` builds all three grids. Open it with `?g=` for the grid and `?f=` for the format:

| `?g=` | Grid | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| `world` | Object over its world | Stamp 01 centred at 68% of the width with equal margins above and below, tilted 1.2 degrees, over its own painting blurred and tinted toward Harbour; light from the top left; quiet shadows. No text added: the stamp carries it. |
| `corner` | One axis, type in a corner | The whole Ferry mark as a window into the rowboats painting, 80% of the width on the 10% axis: both halves read, and the paper gap between them is the channel. (A first version cropped the mark off the edges until it no longer read as the mark.) Headline bottom-left in Ferry Sans Bold with the bleed, one subline that names Ferry, so no logo is needed. |
| `stack` | Centre axis, stacked | The headline, then the arrival painting as a postcard (A6 landscape, thin printed border, tilted 2.5 degrees), with an ARRIVED postmark crossing from the card onto the poster. The postmark says who and when, so there's no mark and no footnote. (A first version, a framed print with a footnote and a small mark, read as a stock mockup and was rejected.) |

`?f=4x5` is 1080 × 1350, `?f=9x16` is 1080 × 1920. In 9:16, the corner grid's mark grows to 1.5 times the width so the window reaches down toward the type, and the stack's zones spread over the extra height.

**Rules the templates hold:**
- 10% axis and margins; headlines about 55 to 65% of the width, never full width.
- Headlines are Ferry Sans Bold at −5% tracking with the watercolour bleed. Bodoni Moda stays on the stamp.
- Every detail says something new: the subline names Ferry, the footnote states the promise as times. Nothing else is added.
- Shadows are the quiet ones from stamp 01; light falls from the top left.
- Paintings: lighthouse (world), rowboats (corner) and a ferry arriving at golden hour (stack, painted in gouache from a Pinterest photo). All three are studies of other people's work, kept in the git-ignored `ferry/assets/refs/`, so the templates only render locally until Ferry has paintings of its own.

**Update (Nov 12, 2026):** the templates above were rejected as weak implementations without a real grid. They're replaced by the Swiss grid system in `SWISS_GRID.md` and `print/swiss.html`: six layouts drawn from the board, on one modular grid, at 4:5 and 16:9. The 9:16 format is dropped.
